# AI and Automation Products Sold to UK Letting and Estate Agents: Competitor Map and Market Gaps (as of November 2026)

> Method note: Research was done with WebSearch only. WebFetch failed (DNS not allowed) for lettings.quest, altosoftware.co.uk, clevaa.com and estateagenttoday.co.uk, so most findings come from search-result summaries rather than full page reads. Most sources are vendor marketing, vendor-written "comparisons", or trade-press write-ups of vendor announcements. Treat all traction and results figures as self-reported unless stated otherwise. Dates are flagged where older than 2026.

## 1. Who sells AI and automation to UK agents now: features, pricing, segment, traction

### Takeaway
The market has split into four layers. (a) The big CRMs (Alto, Street, Reapit, Apex27) have shipped AI lead handling and call answering inside their own product during 2026. (b) The portals (Rightmove, Zoopla) are adding AI that summarises calls and supports compliance. (c) Managed human or hybrid services (Yomdel, Moneypenny) still claim more than 2,500 agency customers each. (d) A long tail of small AI receptionist and chat vendors priced at roughly £49 to £300 a month (Voco, LettingGuru Nita, instep.ai, Yield, Clevaa, VoiceProp, Vocoweb, Softomate). Lettings-specific maintenance triage is dominated by Fixflo plus its Aidenn AI. Funding is going to roll-ups (Dwelly) more than to point tools.

### Inferences
- Rough segment map:
  - Corporates and large multi-branch agencies: Reapit, Rightmove premium tiers and Yomdel.
  - Mid-size and independent agencies: Alto, Street and Moneypenny.
  - Small independents: Apex27 and LettingGuru, the CRMs aimed at smaller firms, plus the £49–£300/month AI receptionist start-ups.
- CRM-native AI is the biggest competitive threat to a solo founder. If an agent's CRM ships "free" or bundled lead handling (Street claims no extra subscription; Alto Lead Flow is sold through the existing contract), a standalone service must win on lettings depth, multichannel follow-up, cross-CRM support or done-for-you service.
- Pricing anchors: AI receptionists cluster at £49–£300/month per agency (not per branch). CRM add-ons run about £149–£150/month plus usage. Custom builds run £2,000+ setup. That leaves room for a managed "AI plus human oversight" offer at roughly £200–£600/month per branch, but it would have to beat Moneypenny and Yomdel on outcomes (viewings booked) rather than on messages taken.

## 2. What agents complain about and where the gaps are

### Takeaway
Direct, independent evidence of agent complaints about AI tools is thin. Complaints that are documented relate to CRM vendors: price rises, poor support and failed migrations, mainly Alto. The strongest gap signals come from three places: slow response times to enquiries, high out-of-hours demand in lettings, tools that have not been updated for the Renters' Rights Act, and segments (student/HMO, block management, small lettings-only firms) served mainly by niche or no AI.

  - an April 2026 price rise paired with weaker support;
  - no clear contractual cap on increases;

  - a migration from Reapit that failed after three attempts.

### Inferences (gaps a solo founder could exploit)
1. **Lettings-first, RRA-aware enquiry handling.** Most AI lead tools are sales-centric: valuation capture is the headline metric for Street, Yield, SalesRook and Rightmove. Under the RRA the lettings scripts have changed: no bidding wars or offers above asking rent, no Section 21, pets requests, and the Information Sheet. A lettings-specific script set (applicant qualification on affordability, Right to Rent, move-in date and pets, plus compliant wording) is a defensible wedge.
2. **CRM-agnostic overlay for agents on CRMs without native AI, or on older contracts.** Alto and Street lock AI to their own CRM, and Reapit's is still rolling out. Agents on Jupix, Dezrez, Expert Agent, Goodlord, Arthur, LetAdmin, or simply spreadsheets and email are under-served. Agents unhappy with CRM price rises may welcome a tool that is not tied to their CRM.
3. **Multichannel follow-up and chasing, not just first response.** Most products focus on answering the first enquiry. The documented failure is response and follow-up (61% replying within 24 hours). WhatsApp/SMS nurture, no-show reduction and viewing feedback loops are less emphasised by the incumbents. Only Alto Lead Flow and instep explicitly mention WhatsApp.
4. **Out-of-hours tenant and maintenance line for small managing agents.** Fixflo data shows 38% of issues and 45% of emergencies are reported out of hours. Contractor callouts out of hours cost up to 51% more, so triage that prevents unnecessary callouts has a clear ROI story. Fixflo with Aidenn is the leader but is a platform buy. A lighter managed out-of-hours voice and WhatsApp triage service for agents managing 50–500 units is plausible.
5. **Landlord updates and reporting.** Rightmove's Property Performance report addresses this for sales listings, but no lettings-focused automated landlord update product (rent arrears, maintenance status, compliance expiry, RRA changes) appeared in the searches. This is a possible white space, though unverified.
6. **Under-served segments.** Small independent and lettings-only firms (the majority of the more than 26,000 agencies) have little access to Rightmove premium AI or Reapit. Also under-served: student/HMO (Hybr is niche), block management (no clear AI leader) and regional agencies outside London.
7. **Done-for-you service plus measurable outcomes.** Agents already pay for human services (Yomdel, Moneypenny), which suggests they value outcomes and hand-holding over software. A solo founder can sell "managed AI receptionist plus monthly results report" on a pay-per-booked-viewing or per-branch basis. This differs from pure SaaS (£49–£150) and undercuts custom builds (£2,000+).
8. **Risks.** CRM bundling (free or included AI) compresses prices. Many look-alike AI automation agencies already target the sector. Dwelly-style roll-ups remove independent lettings agencies from the buyer pool.

### Gaps
- No independent survey of agent satisfaction with AI chat and voice tools was found. Complaint evidence about AI specifically is anecdotal or vendor-sourced.
- No Propertymark, ARLA or UK PropTech Association data found on AI adoption rates.
- No data on how many agencies are lettings-only, or on block management / HMO agency counts.

## 3. Outsourced human services agents pay for: willingness-to-pay benchmarks

### Takeaway
Agents already pay for human cover. Moneypenny-style answering starts at about £49–£55/month and reaches several hundred pounds at higher volume. Yomdel uses per-lead (historically about £10 per qualified lead) or retainer pricing. UK property virtual assistants cost about £18–£40/hour or £480–£2,320/month. Out-of-hours maintenance cover is usually pay-per-call. These set a ceiling of roughly £200–£2,000/month for an AI-led service, depending on scope.

## 4. Evidence of results from case studies

### Takeaway
Every quantified result found is vendor- or consultant-reported, and most come from anonymised agencies. Claimed gains cluster at roughly +23% to +33% viewings, +27% out-of-hours leads, faster response (4 hours to 3 minutes) and +50% valuation enquiries from Rightmove's tool. There are no independent audits.
