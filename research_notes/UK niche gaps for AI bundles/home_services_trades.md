# UK Home Services & Trades Niches for a Bundled "Websites + Marketing + AI Automation" Service (as of Nov 2026)

Research note: about 20 web searches run on 19 Nov 2026. Firecrawl was unavailable, so most findings come from search-result summaries rather than full page fetches. A lot of the trade statistics come from marketing and SaaS vendors (whito.co.uk, trade2base, callout-ai, Softomate, Payaca and others), which have a commercial interest. These are flagged as **[vendor]**. Primary sources (Ofgem, GOV.UK, ICO, ONS via reporters, MCS) are flagged as **[primary]** where relevant. Niches with thin evidence (pest control, removals, locksmiths, landscapers, windows/doors, EV chargers) are said to be thin in their Gaps sections. Their scores are partly judgement and are marked as such.

## Cross-cutting: Lead platforms (Checkatrade, Bark, MyBuilder, Rated People): costs and complaints

### Takeaway
Tradespeople pay roughly £30–£200+/month for Checkatrade, often plus per-lead fees. Bark's credit model gets a lot of complaints about fake or shared leads and credits that do not convert. This "rented pipeline" is the clearest wedge for an "own your lead pipeline" bundle.

### Cited Findings
- Checkatrade's own blog (last updated Dec 2024, **older data**) says the basic plan starts "from £30 + VAT per month" — [Checkatrade blog](https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/trade/start-business/does-checkatrade-cost-money/)
- 2026 third-party estimates: tiers listed as £0 free, £30 "Approved", "from £59/mo" Growth. Typical spend estimated at £800–£2,400/year (£67–£200/month), around £60/month at the basic end, and £120–£180 in competitive areas — [Whito Checkatrade cost](https://whito.co.uk/tools/checkatrade-cost-uk/) **[vendor]**; [Trade2Base "Is Checkatrade worth it 2026"](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/is-checkatrade-worth-it-2026) **[vendor]**
- Members report lead-related fees of roughly £5–£15 for small jobs and £20–£40 for large jobs. Checkatrade now runs a Pay-As-You-Go product — [Trade2Base](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/checkatrade-vs-google-ads-tradespeople) **[vendor]**; [Checkatrade PAYG terms](https://www.checkatrade.com/product-terms-pay-as-you-go)
- Bark: leads cost 3 to more than 20 credits each (about $5–$36 per lead per one review). Common complaints are fake or test leads, refunds given as credits rather than cash, credits expiring, and jobs left open after the customer has hired someone else. G2 average is 1.6/5 from 332 reviews — [G2 Bark reviews](https://g2.com/products/bark-com/reviews?page=10); [Trustpilot UK Bark](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/bark.com?page=8); [Hook Agency](https://hookagency.com/blog/bark-reviews-contractors/)
- Boiler Guide reportedly charges £15–£35 per lead in competitive postcodes (possibly dated) — [Trade2Base boiler installation](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/boiler-installation-business-uk) **[vendor]**
- Daikin sends manufacturer leads only to installers in its "Stand By Me" programme and partners with lead-generation agencies — [Daikin installer guidance](https://www.daikin.co.uk/en_gb/installer/lmt-guidance.html)

### Inferences
- A typical small trade likely spends about £100–£300/month on directories and leads. That is the budget line a bundle can replace or supplement. ROI pitch: "the same spend, but the leads and reviews are yours (GBP plus your own site) and are not shared with 3–5 competitors."
- Reviews kept on Checkatrade or Bark are locked to that platform. A bundle that pushes reviews to Google Business Profile builds an asset the trade owns.

### Gaps
- I could not verify Checkatrade's official 2026 price list, MyBuilder's per-lead "shortlist fee" prices, Rated People's prices, or TrustATrader's prices. No Reddit (r/UKtrades) or Facebook group threads were retrieved.
- No independent, aggregated complaint data for Checkatrade was found. One search returned no complaint sources.

## Cross-cutting: Missed calls and AI call-answering for UK trades (competition and pricing)

### Takeaway
Missed calls are a widely reported pain: about a third of trades say they have lost work by not answering. The UK AI receptionist market for trades is already crowded at £30–£300/month, so call answering on its own is commoditised. The gap is in bundling it with the trade's own lead pipeline and integrating it into quoting, booking and invoicing.

### Cited Findings
- Fix Radio survey of 220+ UK tradespeople: 34% had lost work because they did not answer the phone, and 60% regularly struggle to take calls on site (reported by a secondary blog; original release not found) — [Trade2Base call handling](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/trade-business-call-handling-uk) **[vendor-reported]**
- Everreach survey of 250+ UK small businesses (2024, **older**) estimated plumbers lose about £500/week, or £26,000/year, from missed calls. This is an extrapolation, not a measurement — [HVP Magazine](https://www.hvpmag.co.uk/Missed-calls-cost-plumbers-over-amppound25000-a-year-according-to-survey)
- UBC study (2025) of 142 UK small businesses: 47% of first calls went unanswered — [callout-ai blog](https://callout-ai.pages.dev/blog/cost-of-missed-calls) **[vendor-reported]**
- UK AI receptionist pricing:
  - Fasthosts AI Receptionist from £39/month ex VAT, with 30 calls on the smallest plan and a free first month — [Fasthosts](https://www.fasthosts.co.uk/blog/how-much-does-an-ai-receptionist-cost/)
  - Booking-capable AI receptionist estimated at £59–£199/month; £97 basic up to £297+ with CRM and booking — [Softomate pricing](https://www.softomatesolutions.com/blog/ai-receptionist-pricing-uk/) **[vendor]**; [callout-ai](https://callout-ai.pages.dev/blog/ai-receptionist-cost) **[vendor]**
  - Trades-specific vendor: £149 setup for sole traders, around £149/month — [ScaleLabs](https://scalelabs-studio.pages.dev/blog/ai-receptionist-cost-uk) **[vendor]**
  - Powered Now (a UK trade job-management app) launched "Powered Now Receptionist" — [Powered Now](https://powerednow.com/receptionist/); [Electrical Contracting News](https://electricalcontractingnews.com/?p=53659)
  - Human answering services cost about £50–£200+/month. A full-time receptionist costs £24k–£35k+/year — [callout-ai](https://callout-ai.pages.dev/blog/ai-receptionist-cost) **[vendor]**
- Many small, recent AI-receptionist sites target UK trades (callout-ai, ScaleLabs, softsync-ai, CallSphere, Clara, Halo AI), which suggests heavy indie competition — [softsync-ai](https://softsync-ai.pages.dev/blog-missed-calls-trades); [CallSphere](https://callsphere.ai/blog/smb-uk-home-services-tradespeople-missed-calls.md); [Halo](https://haloaiservices.pages.dev/blog/ai-receptionist-roi-small-business-uk)

### Inferences
- Standalone AI call answering is heading toward commodity pricing (£39–£150/month), and the incumbent job-management apps (Jobber, Tradify, Powered Now, ServiceM8) are adding their own. A founder should not lead with "AI receptionist". The defensible offer is the full workflow: answer, qualify, book a survey, quote, follow up, chase payment, request a review. It needs niche-specific scripts, such as BUS eligibility checks for heat pumps or Gas Safe emergency triage for boilers.

## Cross-cutting: Regulation affecting outreach (PECR) and demand drivers

### Takeaway
Under PECR, sole traders and ordinary partnerships are "individual subscribers": unsolicited marketing emails and texts to them need consent (or soft opt-in, which does not apply to cold prospects). Ltd companies can be cold-emailed with an opt-out. That pushes outreach toward Ltd firms (installers, builders, cleaning companies) and toward phone, in-person and community channels for sole traders.

### Cited Findings
- ICO: corporate subscribers are companies, LLPs and Scottish partnerships. "Sole traders and other types of partnerships are classed as individual subscribers" and are treated the same as individuals. The email/text consent rule does not apply to corporate subscribers — [ICO B2B marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing/business-to-business-marketing); [ICO electronic mail rules](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-pecr/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/what-are-the-rules-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail) **[primary]**
- Live B2B calls to businesses are allowed unless the number is TPS/CTPS-registered or the business has objected (general PECR summary) — [LegalVision](https://legalvision.co.uk/data-privacy-it/electronic-telephone-marketing/)
- From 6 April 2026, sole traders and landlords with income over £50,000 must use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax. This pushes digital-adoption and invoicing-software decisions in 2026 — [Whito/ONS summary](https://whito.co.uk/research/uk-self-employment-statistics/) **[vendor]**
- Future Homes Standard published 24 March 2026. It applies to non-higher-risk projects submitted from 24 March 2027 and is fully in place by about 2028. It effectively bans gas boilers in new homes and requires solar on most new homes — [BRE](https://bregroup.com/insights/future-homes-standard-what-it-means-and-what-comes-next); [Mortgage Solutions](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/green-mortgages/2026/03/24/future-homes-standard-implementation-delayed-until-2028/); [Parliament written statement](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-statements/1894121) **[primary/secondary]**

### Inferences
- Pick Ltd-heavy niches for scalable cold email (MCS installers are mostly Ltd). For sole-trader-heavy niches (plumbers, electricians, locksmiths, cleaners), use cold calling (check TPS/CTPS), Facebook groups, merchants, referrals and content.

### Gaps
- No UK data found on the Ltd vs sole-trader split by trade, apart from the roofing estimate below.

## Niche 1: Heat pump & solar/battery installers (MCS), the highest-priority high-ticket niche

### Takeaway
This is the strongest candidate. Demand is at record levels: BUS applications hit a record 9,738 in Sept 2026, and MCS logged about 36,000 battery and about 150,000 solar installs in H1 2026. Job values are £8k–£15k+. The installer base is small and countable (about 1,800 BUS-registered companies), mostly Ltd, and easy to reach through MCS directories. The pain sits in lead qualification, grant paperwork and long sales cycles. Specialist CRMs exist (Payaca, Spruce, Reonic, Zigaflow), but done-for-you marketing plus AI pre-qualification is open. The main risk is dependence on policy (BUS budget, Warm Homes Plan timing).

### Cited Findings
- **Installer count:** 1,789 MCS-certified installation companies were registered for BUS at end of March 2026. The ten largest accounted for over 36.5% of grant applications in Scheme Year 4 — [Ofgem BUS Annual Report 2025–26](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/transparency-document/boiler-upgrade-scheme-annual-report-april-2025-march-2026) **[primary]**
- MCS had 2,034 certified battery installers and 4,066 certified solar contractors in Jan 2025 (**older**). MCS-certified installers grew 7% during 2025 — [Solar Power Portal](https://www.solarpowerportal.co.uk/battery-storage/mcs-reports-record-january-for-home-renewables-surge-in-battery-storage); [Electrical Times](https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/?p=31649)
- **Demand:**
  - Record 9,738 BUS voucher applications and 6,298 vouchers issued in Sept 2026 — [Selectra](https://selectra.co.uk/energy/news/heat-pump-grant-extra-120m-vouchers-open-until-april-2027) **[secondary; see Ofgem/GOV.UK monthly stats]**
  - 8,150 applications in July 2026, +81% on June — [Eco Experts](https://www.theecoexperts.co.uk/news/record-number-homes-apply-boiler-upgrade-scheme)
  - Official monthly series — [GOV.UK BUS statistics Sept 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/boiler-upgrade-scheme-statistics-september-2026) **[primary]**
- **BUS budget and grants:**
  - £232m spent in Year 4. Ofgem reports £143.6m left for Year 5 (Apr 2026–Mar 2027) — [Ofgem monthly update](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/data/bus-monthly-scheme-update) **[primary]**
  - Up to £520m of vouchers allowed in 2026–27, £120m above the £400m budget — [Selectra](https://selectra.co.uk/energy/news/heat-pump-grant-extra-120m-vouchers-open-until-april-2027)
  - Standard air-source grant £7,500. From 21 July 2026 to 31 March 2027, oil and LPG households can get £9,000. Hybrids not eligible — [Eco Experts BUS](https://www.theecoexperts.co.uk/blog/boiler-upgrade-scheme)
- **Solar and battery:**
  - About 150,000 certified solar installs (+14%) and 36,000 battery installs (nearly double) in H1 2026. 92% of batteries went into homes that already had solar. About 210,000 certified installs in total for H1 2026 — [CIBSE Journal](https://www.cibsejournal.com/?p=47081); [Renewable Energy Magazine](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/pv_solar/record-number-of-uk-households-combining-renewables-20260820)
  - Solar Energy UK counted 207,619 MCS solar installs so far in 2026, calling it a "record year" linked to energy price rises — [Solar Power Portal](https://www.solarpowerportal.co.uk/solar-projects/2026-record-year-for-uk-rooftop-solar-installations-as-iran-war-hikes-energy-prices)
  - More than 2 million UK solar installations — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/05/06/there-are-now-more-than-2-million-uk-solar-installations/)
- **Warm Homes Plan:**
  - £15bn plan published Jan 2026. One guide cites £13.2bn to upgrade up to 5m homes by 2030. It includes zero and low-interest loans for solar, batteries and heat pumps, but loan timing conflicts between sources (Oct 2026 vs 2027) — [Elmhurst Energy](https://www.elmhurstenergy.co.uk/blog/2026/01/21/warm-homes-plan-revealed-15bn-programme-sets-direction-for-home-upgrades-epc-reform-and-rental-standards/); [Reonic installer guide](https://reonic.com/en-gb/blog/warm-homes-plan-installer-guide/); [Heat Geek FAQ](https://www.heatgeek.com/articles/warm-homes-plan-faq-grants-solar-the-new-agency)
  - A reported £2,500 battery grant could not be confirmed from a primary source.
- **ECO4/GBIS:** GBIS closed 31 March 2026. ECO4 extended to 31 December 2026, with no successor supplier obligation announced — [GOV.UK ECO4 extension response](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/extending-the-eco4-end-date/outcome/extending-the-eco4-end-date-government-response-html) **[primary]**
  - The Installation Assurance Authority claims 3,000+ job losses already and 26,000 installer jobs at risk within 6 months (undated, unverified) — [Construction Index](https://www.theconstructionindex.co.uk/news/view/retrofit-supply-chain-fears-cliff-edge-of-eco-cancellation?amp=1); [Building](https://www.building.co.uk/news/the-cliff-edge-has-probably-been-reached--when-the-warm-homes-plan-finally-arrives-will-there-be-an-industry-left-to-deliver-it/5140216.article)
- **Existing software:**
  - Payaca (claims the only heat-pump-specific CRM), Spruce (claims 800+ UK installers), Zigaflow (BUS/MCS paperwork), Commusoft, Reonic — [Payaca](https://payaca.com/uk/industries/heat-pump-installers); [Spruce](https://www.spruce.eco/demo-video-b); [Zigaflow](https://zigaflow.com/industries/renewables/heat-pumps) **[vendor]**
  - An instant-quote tool for installers exists — [ITS Technologies](https://app.itstechnologies.uk/instant-quote)
- **Lead channels:** local search, content, referrals and paid ads, plus MCS, RECC, HPA, TrustMark and local authority listings. Manufacturer lead programmes (Daikin "Stand By Me") — [Payaca lead gen](https://payaca.com/blog/heat-pump-lead-generation); [Commusoft](https://www.commusoft.com/en-gb/blog/heat-pump-leads/); [Daikin](https://www.daikin.co.uk/en_gb/installer/lmt-guidance.html)

### Inferences
- **Job value / LTV (judgement, not sourced):** heat pump installs are roughly £10k–£15k gross before the £7,500 grant. Solar plus battery is roughly £7k–£14k. LTV adds batteries to existing solar (92% of batteries are retrofits to solar homes, so there is an upsell base), annual servicing and referrals. One extra install a month likely covers a £1,000–£1,500/month bundle many times over.
- **Pain:** many low-quality enquiries (renters, ineligible properties, tyre-kickers), heavy survey and design workload, BUS/MCS paperwork, long nurture cycles. Tight voucher budgets and grant deadlines (the oil/LPG £9k window ends 31 Mar 2027) create urgency.
- **Concentration risk:** the top 10 firms win 36.5% of applications, so the roughly 1,750 smaller firms compete for the rest. They are the natural buyers of a bundle that helps them compete.
- **ECO4-dependent installers** face a "cliff edge" in Jan 2027 and may need to pivot to retail (private-pay) demand. That is a timely pitch: "replace ECO funnel with your own retail lead engine".
- **Suggested bundle:** "Installer Growth OS", about £995–£1,495/month plus £1.5k setup:
  - Conversion-focused site with grant and savings calculator
  - Service-area GBP pages and reviews
  - Meta and Google ads management (ad spend extra)
  - AI call/chat agent that pre-qualifies (owner occupier, property type, EPC, current fuel, BUS eligibility) and books remote or home surveys
  - Automated proposal follow-ups
  - Post-install review and referral sequences
  - BUS document checklist chasing
  - ROI: 1 extra job at about £2k–£4k gross margin per month pays for it.
- **SaaS potential: high.** AI enquiry-to-survey qualification and grant-workflow automation for MCS installers is a clear vertical product. It could plug into or compete with Payaca and Spruce.
- **Reachability: strong.** Public MCS installer search, BUS-registered firms, InstallerSHOW (NEC, annual, June), Heat Geek community, manufacturer training events (Vaillant, Daikin, Mitsubishi), LinkedIn. Mostly Ltd, so PECR-compliant cold email to corporate addresses is possible.
- **Scores (1–5):** Demand/pain 5 · Ability to pay 5 · Competition gap 3 (specialist CRMs exist; done-for-you growth plus AI is less covered) · Reachability 5 · Recurring/scalability 4 · Regulatory risk 3 (policy and grant budget dependence; reasonably stable to 2028 via BUS and the Warm Homes Plan, but ECO end and loan timing are uncertain)

### Gaps
- No primary-source average job value or installer revenue figures. No 2026 MCS installer total. Marketing spend per installer is unknown. Warm Homes Plan loan launch date conflicts between sources.

## Niche 2: Heating engineers / boiler installers (Gas Safe)

### Takeaway
This is a large base: the main Gas Safe trade magazine reaches 77,000+ engineers. Pain is high around missed emergency calls and lead platforms (Boiler Guide £15–£35/lead). Boiler swaps are mid-to-high ticket (judgement: about £2.5k–£4.5k), and annual servicing is the recurring core. The Future Homes Standard (2027–28) and heat-pump policy push gas engineers toward low-carbon upskilling, which creates a secondary marketing need.

### Cited Findings
- Registered Gas Engineer magazine is distributed to 77,000+ gas engineers across the UK. This is a distribution figure, not a register count — [RGE magazine Jan/Feb 2026](https://registeredgasengineer.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/RGE_Jan-Feb26_DIGITAL-optimised.pdf)

- Boiler Guide lead pricing £15–£35 in competitive postcodes — [Trade2Base](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/boiler-installation-business-uk) **[vendor]**
- Plumbers (which overlaps heavily with heating) estimated to lose about £26k/year from missed calls (2024 extrapolation) — [HVP Magazine](https://www.hvpmag.co.uk/Missed-calls-cost-plumbers-over-amppound25000-a-year-according-to-survey)
- Future Homes Standard effectively removes gas boilers from new builds from 2027–28 — [BRE](https://bregroup.com/insights/future-homes-standard-what-it-means-and-what-comes-next)
- Commusoft is a long-standing UK heating/gas job-management platform — [Commusoft](https://www.commusoft.com/en-gb/blog/heat-pump-leads/)

### Inferences
- Bundle about £350–£600/month:
  - Emergency-triage AI answering (no heat / no hot water / gas smell → safety script and escalation)
  - Online boiler-quote calculator with photo upload
  - GBP and reviews
  - Annual-service reminder automation, which is high-margin recurring work
  - Invoice chasing
  - ROI: 2 extra boiler swaps a year, or roughly 30 retained service customers, covers it.
- Bridge to Niche 1: gas engineers moving into heat pumps need MCS-oriented marketing.
- Online fixed-price boiler sellers (Boxt, Heatable, etc.) compete for consumer search. This is judgement and was not researched here.
- **Scores:** Demand/pain 4 · Ability to pay 4 · Competition gap 3 · Reachability 4 (merchants such as Plumb Center and City Plumbing, Gas Safe-heavy Facebook groups, RGE magazine; many sole traders, so PECR limits cold email) · Recurring 4 (servicing) · Regulatory risk 4

### Gaps

## Niche 3: Roofers

### Takeaway
There are about 12,900 VAT/PAYE roofing businesses (ONS, Mar 2026), 94.8% with fewer than 10 staff. Roughly half of roofers are estimated to be sole traders. Roofing is high-ticket: full replacements run £6.5k–£14.5k+ per Bestbuilders, and the FMB says "from £13k". Agencies already target roofers heavily (SEO £500–£1,800/month), so there is competition. Solar-on-roof is a crossover.

### Cited Findings
- 12,895 VAT/PAYE roofing businesses in March 2026 (ONS); 94.8% employ fewer than 10 — [Whito UK roofing industry](https://whito.co.uk/roofers/uk-roofing-industry/) **[vendor citing ONS]**
- Roughly 50–55% of UK roofers are self-employed sole traders (single-site estimate, unverified) — same source
- Roof replacement: FMB "from £13,000"; Bestbuilders £6,500–£14,500 for a 3-bed semi — [FMB](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/how-much-does-a-house-renovation-cost.html); [Bestbuilders](https://www.bestbuilders.co.uk/guides/costs)

## Niche 14: Damp & mould specialists (Awaab's Law)

### Takeaway
Awaab's Law has applied to damp and mould since 27 Oct 2025, and Phase 2 widens hazards from 30 Nov 2026. It imposes strict timescales on social landlords (investigate within 10 working days, make safe within 5). That creates B2B contractor demand from housing associations and councils. Winning that work depends on procurement frameworks, response-time SLAs and evidence reporting, not consumer marketing. The bundle fit is a workflow and compliance-evidence SaaS rather than websites and ads.

### Cited Findings
- Phase 1 from 27 Oct 2025: investigate significant damp and mould within 10 working days, make safe within 5 working days after the investigation — [Contact](https://contact.org.uk/about-contact/news-and-views/awaabs-law-rules-to-protect-social-housing-tenants-in-england-from-damp-and-unsafe-homes/); [HCR Law](https://www.hcrlaw.com/news-and-insights/navigating-the-reality-of-awaabs-law-year-one-in-social-housing/) **[legal commentary]**
- Phase 2 from 30 Nov 2026 adds 7 hazard categories, including electrical. Phase 3 in 2027 covers the remaining hazards except overcrowding. Applies to about 4m social housing tenants in England — [Shelter](https://england.shelter.org.uk/professional_resources/news_and_updates/whats_changing_under_phase_2_of_awaabs_law); [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/08/awaab-s-law-phase-2-the-next-compliance-challenge); [ITV](https://www.itv.com/news/granada/2026-07-13/safer-homes-for-four-million-tenants-with-quicker-action-on-dangerous-houses)
- Government Test and Learn report: the law is starting to shift behaviour, but tenant awareness is low — [GOV.UK Test and Learn summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-phase-1-test-and-learn-research-report/awaabs-law-test-and-learn-research-report-executive-summary-accessible-version) **[primary]**
- Private landlords are preparing for possible extension (Renters' Rights context) — [Frosts guide](https://www.frosts.co.uk/blog/awaabs-law-guide-private-landlords)
- Contractor-specific demand or pricing data: none found.

### Inferences
- The opening is a B2B workflow product: AI intake of tenant reports, photo triage, SLA clock tracking, evidence packs for landlords. This competes with housing-repairs platforms more than with trade marketing. The homeowner-facing damp market (surveys, PIV units) suits a lighter consumer bundle.
- **Scores:** Demand/pain 4 (regulatory push) · Ability to pay 3 · Competition gap 3 · Reachability 2 (procurement frameworks; housing associations) · Recurring 4 · Regulatory risk 3 (the rules help demand, but this is a compliance-critical workflow carrying liability)

### Gaps
- No count of damp specialists. No contractor pricing or capacity data. Phase 2's statutory hazard list not verified from legislation.
