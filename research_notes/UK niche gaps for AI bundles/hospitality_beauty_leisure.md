## Cross-cutting: regulatory drivers and constraints

### Takeaway
Three regulatory changes act as sales triggers: England's short-term-let register (now expected **March 2027**), FHL tax abolition (April 2025), and the tipping code revision (late 2026). None of them blocks the service. PECR's soft opt-in is the main constraint on retention SMS/email campaigns, and it is manageable.

### Cited Findings
- **England STL registration:** updated government guidance (2 Oct 2026) says the mandatory registration scheme for short-term lets is now expected to start in **March 2027**. It is made under s.228 of the Levelling Up and Regeneration Act 2023 and covers anyone letting self-catering property or short-term accommodation for leisure — [Clyde & Co, Oct 2026](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/10/england-s-short-let-registration-scheme-what-the-m); [Propertymark](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/resource/national-short-term-lets-register-promised-by-march-2027.html); [The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/rental-market/date-revealed-for-launch-of-new-short-term-and-holiday-lets-register/). Earlier guides that cite spring/April 2026 are stale. A July 2026 guide reported no portal and no commencement date as of 29 Jul 2026 — [GuestReady](https://www.guestready.com/?p=12453).
- **FHL regime abolished** on 6 April 2025 for income tax and CGT, and on 1 April 2025 for corporation tax — [GOV.UK policy paper](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/furnished-holiday-lettings-tax-regime-abolition/abolition-of-the-furnished-holiday-lettings-tax-regime). Owners lose full mortgage-interest relief and BADR (10% CGT). Accountants report materially higher 2025/26 bills for mortgaged owners, and some owners are switching to long lets or selling — [SJP, Mar 2026](https://www.sjp.co.uk/individuals/news/owners-urged-to-review-holiday-lets-as-tax-changes-bite); [Keystone Law](https://www.keystonelaw.com/keynotes/the-abolition-of-the-furnished-holiday-lettings-fhl-regime); [Whitings LLP](https://whitingsllp.co.uk/airbnb-and-the-end-of-furnished-holiday-lets/). Making Tax Digital for landlords above set thresholds starts on 6 April 2027 — [learnformula](https://learnformula.com/latest-intelligence/the-fhl-hangover-untangling-the-furnished-holiday-let-abolition-in-the-202526-tax-return).
- **Tipping law:** the correct name is the *Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act **2023***, in force since **1 Oct 2024** with a statutory code. Employment tribunals must take the code into account, and tronc schemes are permitted — [Charles Russell Speechlys](https://www.charlesrussellspeechlys.com/en/insights/expert-insights/employment/2024/employment-allocation-of-tips-act-2023-now-in-effect/); [GOV.UK news](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/millions-to-take-home-more-cash-as-tipping-laws-come-into-force). The Employment Rights Act 2025 will require a **written tipping policy**. The consultation on a revised code closed on 29 Sep 2026, and the revised code is expected to come into force later in 2026 — [Doyle Clayton](https://doyleclayton.co.uk/employment-rights-act-2025-upcoming-changes-to-tips-and-gratuities-legislation/); [CIPP, Aug 2026](https://www.cipp.org.uk/resources/news/consultation-on-revised-tipping-code-of-practice.html); [GOV.UK revised code](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/distributing-tips-fairly-revised-statutory-code-of-practice). The Act applies to restaurants, salons and barbers with employees.
- **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025** became law on 19 June 2025 and amends PECR, extending the soft opt-in to charities — [Penningtons](https://www.penningtonslaw.com/insights/uk-data-protection-law-changes-requirements-to-address/).

### Inferences
- **PECR [background knowledge – not verified this session]:** most targets here are B2B sellers to **consumers**, so PECR applies to the *end-customer* messages the bundle sends (rebooking SMS, win-back email). The soft opt-in covers existing customers for similar services, provided opt-out is offered at collection and in every message. Sole-trader *prospects* of the founder count as "individual subscribers", so the founder's own cold email/SMS outreach to sole traders needs consent. Cold calling (screened against TPS) and DMs are safer outreach channels. DUAA 2025 also raised PECR fines to UK GDPR levels **[background knowledge – verify with ICO]**.
- **Scotland STL licensing [background knowledge – not verified this session]:** a mandatory licence has applied under the Civic Government (Scotland) Act 1982 (Licensing of Short-term Lets) Order 2022 since Oct 2022 for new hosts and 1 Oct 2023 for existing hosts. Edinburgh also has a control area. This cuts supply and raises admin; it does not cut demand for marketing.
- **Natasha's Law [background knowledge – not verified this session]:** full ingredient and allergen labelling on prepacked-for-direct-sale (PPDS) food has been required since 1 Oct 2021. It is a constraint on any AI ordering or chat bot for restaurants: the bot must never make allergen assurances and must hand off to staff.
- The STL register (March 2027) is an **outreach hook**: "get compliant + get direct bookings". Every registered property will probably end up on a public or semi-public list, which could become a prospect list.

### Gaps
- No primary ICO or legislation.gov.uk page fetched for PECR or Scotland STL; confirm.
- Fee level and data-publication details of the England STL register were not confirmed.
- England's proposed licensing of non-surgical cosmetics (lash/brow/aesthetics adjacent) and local-authority tattoo registration were not researched this session.

## Niche 1: Wedding & events venues (high-ticket enquiries)

### Takeaway
This is the highest ticket per enquiry in the scope: venue hire averages about **£6k** inside a **£20.6–22k** wedding. Venues answer slowly and follow up too little, which matches the bundle's AI enquiry, follow-up and viewing-booking automation closely. The market is small (thousands of venues, not tens of thousands) but has high ability to pay. Strong candidate.

### Cited Findings
- Average UK wedding cost in 2026: about **£21,990** (Hitched, 2,020 newlyweds), with spend per guest up 4% to **£272** — [The Knot Worldwide/Hitched press release, Jan 2026](https://www.theknotww.com/press-releases/the-average-cost-of-a-wedding-in-2026-around-21990-according-to-hitched). Bridebook 2026 gives **£20,604**, with venue hire excluding catering at **£6,040** (7,000 couples) — [Bridebook](https://bridebook.com/uk/article/national-wedding-survey-2018). The Hitched venue figure of £6,200 (28%) comes only from a secondary blog — [Weddings Hub (unverified)](https://weddings-hub.pages.dev/articles/average-uk-wedding-cost-2026-hitched-vs-bridebook/).
- Response-time guidance: acknowledge within about **15 minutes** and send a tailored proposal within 24 business hours — [Indigo Digital (agency, vendor content)](https://indigodigital.com/insights/real-cost-of-a-missed-enquiry-response-time). A UK venue consultant says a week's delay is common and unacceptable — [Wedinspire](https://wedinspire.com/articles/business/success-guide-to-responding-to-venue-enquiries-leads/).
- Venues follow up on average only **~1.4 times**, while 3–4 follow-ups are often needed to get a reply (Wedinspire survey) — [Wedinspire](https://www.wedinspire.com/articles/business/guide-to-follow-up-emails/).
- Most venues don't track first-response time — [Coco Wedding Venues](https://cocoweddingvenues.co.uk/our-members-community/understanding-why-your-conversion-rate-has-changed-with-customer-journey-maps/).
- Couples often enquire out of hours, so after-hours response matters (Australian source) — [Real Weddings AU](https://www.realweddings.com.au/rw/blog/after-hours-problem-when-couples-actually-enquire-and-why-it-changes-everything/).
- **Existing software and marketplaces:** Bridebook runs paid venue tiers (including "VenuePro") with a 60-day free Essentials trial and then a 12-month plan, but public pages show "£XXX" placeholders rather than prices — [Bridebook partners pricing](https://partners.bridebook.com/uk/pricing); [Bridebook support](https://support.bridebook.com/en/bridebook-paid-membership-tiers-benefits). Moposa offers enquiry automation for venues — [Moposa](https://www.moposa.com/insider-view/automate-your-wedding-enquiries-3-ways-to-speed-up-your-response-times). Niche agencies such as Indigo Digital (venue lead benchmarks) and the Coco Wedding Venues membership community already exist — [Indigo Digital](https://indigodigital.com/insights/wedding-venue-lead-benchmarks-guide).

### Inferences
- **ROI math:** if one extra wedding a year is worth about £6k in venue hire (Bridebook) and catering or package typically adds more, a **£500–900/month** bundle (£6–11k/yr) pays back with roughly 1–2 extra weddings a year. That is an easy pitch if response time and follow-up are visibly poor.
- **Gap:** marketplaces (Hitched, Bridebook) generate leads but don't run the venue's own speed-to-lead, nurture sequences, viewing booking, WhatsApp/IG replies or "date still available?" chasers. Venue CRMs like Moposa exist but are not paired with website, SEO and ads services. Corporate and party events at the same venues add year-round demand that offsets wedding seasonality.
- **Suggested bundle (est.):** £750–1,200 setup plus **£600–900/month**. It would cover a conversion-optimised website with pricing/brochure gating, GBP and SEO, an AI enquiry agent (web, IG, WhatsApp, Bridebook/Hitched email parsing), a 3–4-touch follow-up sequence, show-round booking, open-day campaigns, and a review engine.
- **SaaS potential:** good. "Speed-to-lead + nurture for venues" is a well-defined workflow. The market is small, though, so wedding venues alone may cap SaaS revenue.
- **Scores:** Demand/pain 4, Ability to pay 5, Competition gap 3, Reachability 4 (venues are listed publicly on Hitched/Bridebook), Recurring/scalability 3, Regulatory risk 5.

### Gaps
- UK venue count not found. Hitched and Bridebook list several thousand, but no verified figure was retrieved.
- No UK enquiry-to-booking conversion benchmark found.
- Bridebook and Hitched venue subscription prices are not public.
- Venue marketing spend per month not found.

## Niche 2: Holiday lets / serviced accommodation / small hotels & B&Bs

### Takeaway
Pain is strong and rising: OTA commissions, FHL tax hit, the STL register from March 2027 and the Scotland licence. PMS and direct-booking platforms (Lodgify, Guesty, Hostaway) already sell booking websites cheaply, so the gap is **marketing that drives traffic to the direct site** plus AI guest messaging, not the booking engine itself. Margins are squeezed, so price sensitivity is high among single-property owners. Better targets are multi-unit operators (5–50 units) and small hotels.

### Cited Findings
- FHL abolition raises tax for mortgaged owners, and some are exiting or switching to long lets (see the regulatory section) — [SJP](https://www.sjp.co.uk/individuals/news/owners-urged-to-review-holiday-lets-as-tax-changes-bite).
- STL register expected March 2027 — [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/10/england-s-short-let-registration-scheme-what-the-m).
- **Software pricing (comparison sites, many competitor-run, treat as indicative):** Lodgify from about **$30/mo**, with conflicting claims of a 1.9% booking fee versus no commission — [G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/lodgify-vs-pricelabs-vs-guesty); [Zeevou (competitor)](https://zeevou.com/?p=181391). Guesty from about **$39/mo** for 1–3 listings, custom above; its websites are said to be commission-free — [ainora.lt](https://ainora.lt/blog/guesty-vs-hostaway-vs-lodgify-comparison-2026). Hostaway is quote-based, with one source citing **€23–44 per listing/month**, and markets direct bookings to cut OTA commissions — [Zeevou (competitor)](https://zeevou.com/?p=101697).
- UKHospitality forecast **574 hotel closures** in 2026 if business rates rises go ahead without relief — [The Caterer, Jan 2026](https://www.thecaterer.com/news/ukhospitality-warns-of-2076-closures-in-2026).

### Inferences
- **[background knowledge – not verified this session]** Booking.com commission is typically about 15% (higher with visibility boosters). Airbnb moved most PMS-connected hosts to host-only fees of about 15–15.5% in late 2025. For a property earning £40k/yr gross on OTAs, moving 20% of bookings direct saves roughly £1.2k/yr in commission, which justifies about £100–250/month only. Pricing must scale per unit.
- **Gap:** the booking engine is commoditised. Under-served areas are (a) repeat-guest email/SMS retention (PECR soft opt-in on past guests), (b) GBP/local SEO for "cottages in X", (c) AI WhatsApp/IG pre-booking Q&A, (d) STL register and compliance-ready content. Small hotels and B&Bs also need restaurant and event enquiry handling.
- **Suggested bundle (est.):** £150/month plus £25/unit for owner-operators. For small hotels and operators with 10+ units, **£400–700/month** including a direct-booking site on top of their PMS, a repeat-guest CRM, AI guest messaging, review management and Meta retargeting.
- **SaaS potential:** medium. The space is crowded with PMS vendors that bundle AI messaging, but a "direct-booking growth" layer that sits on top of a PMS through its API is plausible.
- **Scores:** Demand/pain 4, Ability to pay 3 (squeezed; better for multi-unit operators), Competition gap 2, Reachability 4 (listings are public; the register may make them more so), Recurring/scalability 4, Regulatory risk 3 (moving rules, and supply may shrink).

### Gaps
- UK holiday-let and B&B counts not retrieved.
- Booking.com and Airbnb UK commission rates not verified this session.
- Lodgify, Guesty and Hostaway GBP prices not confirmed.

## Niche 3: Independent restaurants / takeaways

### Takeaway
The pain is acute (delivery commissions of roughly 14–35%, rates rises, closures), but ability to pay is the weakest of all the niches. The market is saturated with ordering-platform vendors (Flipdish, Slerp, Orderyoyo, Toast and EPOS add-ons) and reservations tools (ResDiary, OpenTable, SevenRooms). High churn, tight margins and allergen liability for AI make this a poor first niche, except perhaps for higher-end independents running events and private dining.

### Cited Findings
- Delivery commissions: Just Eat about **14%** with self-delivery and about **30–32%** with its couriers. Deliveroo about **20–35%** depending on plan. Uber Eats **25–30%** with delivery and about 13% for pickup. All figures are vendor or third-party estimates with no official rate cards — [DineOpen (vendor)](https://www.dineopen.com/blog/deliveroo-vs-just-eat-vs-uber-eats-commission-2026); [3S-POS (vendor)](https://www.3s-pos.com/blogs/which-uk-food-delivery-platform-is-best-suited-for-your-restaurant); [Whito (inconsistent tables)](https://whito.co.uk/takeaways/guides/takeaway-delivery-app-commission-uk/).
- UKHospitality forecast **2,076 closures in 2026** (about six a day: 963 restaurants, 574 hotels, 540 pubs) without business rates relief. Some outlets misreport the restaurant figure as 293 — [The Caterer](https://www.thecaterer.com/all-content/ukhospitality-warns-of-2076-closures-in-2026).
- 1,602 accommodation and food-service companies became insolvent in H1 2026, down 6.1% year on year but historically high — [Morning Advertiser, Aug 2026](https://www.morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2026/08/19/hospitality-insolvencies-remain-high-despite-6-fall-in-business-closures/).
- Which? has reported consumer price premiums on delivery apps — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-hidden-costs-of-the-food-delivery-revolution-the-surprising-premium-added-to-your-next-takeaway-a9kAZ8K7lB30).

### Inferences
- **ROI:** a takeaway doing £8k/month on apps at 30% pays about £2.4k/month in commission. Shifting 25% to direct ordering (at about 2–5% platform cost) saves roughly £500/month, so a **£150–300/month** bundle can be justified, but owners are price-sensitive and the competition is many direct-ordering vendors.
- **Gap:** there are many commission-free ordering vendors and EPOS players, but few that combine GBP, reviews, a loyalty/SMS win-back programme and AI phone answering for bookings. AI phone answering for reservations is the strongest wedge for sit-down restaurants (missed calls during service).
- **Tipping Act / ERA 2025** written-policy requirement is a small content or compliance add-on, not a core driver.
- **Scores:** Demand/pain 4, Ability to pay 2, Competition gap 2, Reachability 4, Recurring/scalability 3 (high churn), Regulatory risk 3 (allergen liability for AI, tipping).

### Gaps
- No ResDiary, OpenTable or SevenRooms UK pricing retrieved this session.
- UK restaurant and takeaway counts (ONS IDBR) not retrieved.
- No restaurant marketing spend per month data.

## Niche 4: Hair & beauty salons; barbers; nail/lash/brow

### Takeaway
This is a large market: about **50,400 businesses** and £6.1bn turnover; about **58% self-employed**; more than 80% employ fewer than five people; about 64% turn over under £99k. Booking software is mature and cheap (Fresha, Booksy, Treatwell, Phorest, Timely) and increasingly bundles marketing. Margins are weak: only 40% report a profit. The gap is narrow: IG/WhatsApp DM conversion and rebooking/win-back for **multi-chair salons and premium independents**. Most sole-trader nail, lash and brow techs cannot afford £300+/month.

### Cited Findings
- About 58% of hair and beauty workers are self-employed (60.5% in 2023, 63% in 2021) — [NHBF 2024 stats sheet](https://www.nhbf.co.uk/documents/industry-statistics-2024-sheet/a5-industry-statistics-sheet-2024.pdf). More than 80% of businesses employ fewer than five people and 95% fewer than ten; about 64% turn over under £99k — [NHBF industry statistics](https://www.nhbf.co.uk/advice-and-resources/hair-and-beauty-industry-statistics).
- 50,400 hair and beauty businesses, £6.1bn turnover (NHBF June 2026 baseline, via secondary summary) — [NHBF stats page](https://www.nhbf.co.uk/advice-and-resources/hair-and-beauty-industry-statistics); a second secondary source cites ONS March 2025 — [Policybee](https://policybee.co.uk/blog/uk-hair-and-beauty-industry-statistics).
- NHBF April 2025: 40% profitable, 39% breaking even, 21% loss-making. Labour is about 60% of costs. 75.4% reported worsening recruitment (Autumn 2025) — [NHBF State of Industry Jan 2025](https://nhbf.co.uk/documents/nhbf-state-of-the-industry-summary-jan-2025-final/nhbf-state-of-the-industry-summary-jan-2025-final.pdf); [NHBF costs report](https://nhbf.co.uk/documents/straightening-out-the-costs-report-2025/straightening-out-the-costs-report.pdf).
- **No-shows:** only 8% of UK salons never experience cancellations or no-shows (Fresha, Feb 2026, vendor) — [Fresha](https://www.fresha.com/blog/cancellations-cost-study). Measured hair-salon no-show rate from 3m bookings: **3.16%** (Fresha). Self-reported loss: about **7% of monthly revenue** (booking-platform-funded survey). Average missed appointment about **£39** — [Whito (vendor-funded)](https://whito.co.uk/beauty-salons/beauty-salon-industry-data-report/). £1.2bn a year lost (Powered by Flossie, vendor) — [Scratch](https://www.scratchmagazine.co.uk/news/failure-to-digitise-sees-uk-beauty-industry-lose-1-2-billion). Measured and self-reported figures conflict, so treat no-show pain as moderate where deposits are enforced.
- **Software pricing (Whito, read Sep 2026; Zenoti May 2026):** Fresha **£14.95/mo** for a solo business or **£9.95 per team member**, plus a **20%** new-client marketplace fee (£4 minimum). Booksy **£40/mo** plus £5 per extra user, and 30% Boost fee. Treatwell **35%** on a new client's first booking (about 42% including VAT). Phorest is quote-only — [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/beauty-salons/tools/salon-booking-software-costs/); [Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/en-uk/thecheckin/best-salon-software-uk-2026). Fresha pricing sources conflict, so verify on the live page.

### Inferences
- **Gap:** booking, deposits and basic SMS reminders are solved cheaply, so no-show reduction alone won't sell. Under-served areas: (1) converting Instagram and WhatsApp DM enquiries to bookings with AI (techs answer DMs between clients), (2) owning new-client acquisition through GBP and SEO to avoid Treatwell's 35% and Fresha's 20% marketplace fees, (3) lapsed-client win-back, (4) recruitment marketing, given the staffing crisis.
- **ROI:** for a 4–6 chair salon, ten avoided marketplace new-client fees a month at about £15–25 each, plus recovering 2% of revenue from lapsed clients, can justify **£250–400/month**. For sole-trader nail, lash and brow techs, only about **£49–99/month** productised tiers are realistic. The low price, high volume and high churn there suit SaaS rather than done-for-you service.
- **Barbers:** mostly walk-in or Booksy-driven with low ticket prices. Weak for a service bundle; better as a SaaS self-serve tier.
- **Scores (salons, multi-chair):** Demand/pain 3, Ability to pay 3, Competition gap 2, Reachability 5 (Instagram, very visible), Recurring/scalability 4, Regulatory risk 4. **Barbers:** 3/2/2/5/3/5. **Nail/lash/brow sole traders:** 3/1/2/5/3/4 (cosmetics licensing is pending).

### Gaps
- UK barbershop count not found, nor separate nail, lash and brow counts.
- Average transaction value by service type and client lifetime value not found from a reliable source.
- Salon marketing spend per month not found.
- Timely and Phorest current UK prices and AI features not retrieved.

## Niche 5: Gyms & boutique fitness; personal trainers; yoga/pilates; martial arts/dance schools

### Takeaway
UK fitness is at record levels: **12.2m members, 18% penetration** (ukactive 2026), with low-cost chains expanding. Chains are not targets. Independent and boutique studios, martial arts and dance schools, and PT studios are, because recurring memberships give a clear LTV to market against. Software (Mindbody, Glofox, TeamUp, ClassPass) is mature and adding AI. The gap is lead-to-trial conversion (IG/WhatsApp), trial-to-member follow-up and churn win-back. Martial arts and dance schools are notably under-served and parent-driven.

### Cited Findings
- 12.2m members, 18% penetration of over-16s (ukactive/LeisureDB 2026 market report) — [PAF Media](https://www.paf-media.co.uk/uk-fitness-sector-hits-record-122-million-members); [ukactive](https://ukactive.com/?p=24470).
- LeisureDB 2025: 7,202 gyms, 4,727 private clubs, about 7.8m private members. LeisureDB counts **exclude boutique and PT studios** — [Health Club Management](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-features/Research-Record-highs/38441); [Leisure Opportunities](https://www.leisureopportunities.co.uk/news/Industry-metrics-are-at-an-all-time-high-says-LeisureDB-David-Minton/356016).
- 884 HVLP (low-cost) clubs in Jan 2026, about 17% of private clubs but about 42% of private members. PwC sees room for 1,500–1,750 — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/uk-can-nearly-double-number-of-hvlp-low-price-gyms-pwc-report/). PureGym added 59 net sites in the year to Mar 2026.
- **Software:** TeamUp from about **£83/mo** (Capterra UK), priced by active customers — [Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/150357/teamup). Mindbody does not publish UK pricing (US Starter about $79–99) and includes weekly "AI Summaries" — [G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/mindbody-vs-teamup); [Mindbody (vendor)](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/business/education/blog/2026-studio-software-comparison-mindbody-vs-glofox). Glofox markets "AI Agents" and an AI churn predictor — [vibefam](https://vibefam.com/glofox-review-pricing-features-pros-cons-2026/); [Glofox](https://www.glofox.com/blog/tag/community-tips).

### Inferences
- **ROI:** a boutique member at about £100–150/month staying about 8–12 months is worth roughly £1k+ (estimate, not sourced). Two to four extra members a month justifies **£400–600/month**. Martial arts and dance schools have long-tenure child members, so LTV is high and parents respond well to WhatsApp.
- **Gap:** incumbent software focuses on scheduling and payments. AI agents are emerging (Glofox), but owners still need ads, landing pages, a trial-offer funnel, fast DM response and reviews. Gym-focused agencies (lead-gen "trial offer" funnels, often on GoHighLevel) exist and are fairly competitive. Martial arts and dance are less contested in the UK.
- **Personal trainers:** usually sole traders on low budgets, so a SaaS tier rather than a service bundle.
- **Scores:** Boutique/independent gyms: Demand 4, Pay 3, Gap 2, Reach 4, Recurring 4, Reg 5. Martial arts/dance schools: 4/3/3/4/4/4 (safeguarding and children's data). Yoga/pilates: 3/2/2/4/4/5. PTs: 3/1/2/5/3/5.

### Gaps
- UK counts not found for boutique studios, PTs (CIMSPA/REPs), yoga/pilates studios and martial arts/dance schools.
- Glofox and ClassPass UK prices not retrieved.
- No marketing spend data for independent studios.

## Niche 6: Tattoo studios

### Takeaway
There is almost no verified data. The niche is high-ticket and appointment-based, enquiries come heavily through Instagram DMs (design consults, deposits, aftercare), and it is lightly served by software. It is regulated by local-authority registration. A potentially interesting niche, but unproven.

### Cited Findings
- No UK tattoo studio count or market data found this session.

### Inferences
- DM triage (style, placement, size, budget, deposit) is a natural fit for AI enquiry handling, and artists' books are often managed manually. Ability to pay is uncertain: many artists are self-employed chair-renters.
- **Scores (low confidence):** Demand 3, Pay 3, Gap 4, Reach 5, Recurring 2 (repeat cycles are infrequent), Reg 4.

### Gaps
- Studio count, average ticket, software usage and regulation (local registration, England's licensing plans) all need research.

## Niche 7: Golf & leisure clubs

### Takeaway
Members are rising (750,071 England Golf club members in 2025, a 13-year high) and there are about 1,735 clubs in England. Clubs have established software (IntelligentGolf, BRS, ClubV1 **[background knowledge]**). The gap is marketing for membership acquisition and functions/events (weddings, corporate golf days), which overlaps with Niche 1. Committee-run clubs make sales slow.

### Cited Findings
- 750,071 club members in 2025 (up 2.66%) and junior membership up 34% to 61,483. ## Summary ranking & recommendation

### Takeaway
On the evidence gathered, **wedding & events venues** score best for a solo founder's first done-for-you bundle (highest ticket, clear speed-to-lead gap). **Multi-unit holiday-let operators / small hotels** come second, with regulatory sales hooks. **Martial arts/dance schools** and **boutique studios** have strong recurring LTV. Beauty and barbers have the largest volume and suit a later SaaS self-serve tier. Restaurants and takeaways rank last on ability to pay.
