# UK professional-services niches for a "websites + marketing + AI automation" recurring bundle (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: 17 tool calls (WebSearch summaries plus one failed WebFetch). The WebSearch tool returns summarised snippets, so each citation below points to the result page the summary attributed the fact to. Primary pages were mostly not opened in full; spot-check key numbers before publishing. whito.co.uk could not be fetched: the host is outside this session's allowed network, and it would have to be allowed in network settings. Several useful Whito figures came only through search summaries. Vendor-reported or vendor-survey figures are marked **[vendor]**. Data older than 2026 is marked **[older]**.

## 1. Accountants & bookkeepers (deepest section, the most promising niche)

### Takeaway
This is the strongest niche. MTD for Income Tax went live on 6 April 2026, Companies House ID verification ended its transition on 17/18 November 2026, and firms report capacity crunches. Together these create a measurable, recurring client-chasing and onboarding burden. The top obstacle to on-time MTD quarterly updates is "getting information from clients" (67%), which is exactly the work a document-chasing and AI follow-up bundle does. Practice-management tools (Karbon, Ignition, Content Snare) cover pieces of this. A done-for-you bundle that also covers marketing and website appears to be an open space for small practices (inference).

### Cited Findings

- An earlier Rapport Digital analysis found a drop of 3,745 firms since 2019 (-8.6%), from 43,720 to 39,975 **[older/methodology differs]** — [PQ Magazine](https://www.pqmagazine.com/the-lost-firms/). Treat the count as roughly 40,000 firms (ONS business counts, SIC 69.20).

After that, Companies House may reject filings and block appointments or incorporations — [AccountingWEB "Two weeks, one deadline"](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/232587); [LearnSignal](https://www.learnsignal.com/blog/companies-house-identity-verification-deadline-2026/); [GOV.UK campaign](https://changestoukcompanylaw.campaign.gov.uk/identity-verification/)

- Credas markets ACSP/ID-verification tooling to accountants **[vendor]** — [Credas](https://credas.com/news/why-accountants-should-register-as-an-acsp-ahead-of-the-companies-house-deadline/)

### Inferences
- **The gap:** Karbon, Ignition and Content Snare are self-serve tools the firm must configure and run, priced per seat in USD. Small practices (1–10 staff) that lack capacity, per the TaxCalc and Advancetrack figures, are the ones least able to implement them. A done-for-you service is under-served: it would set up and run the chasing sequences (MTD quarterly records, ID-verification status, year-end records), an AI enquiry and qualification bot on the website, Google Business Profile and reviews, and MTD-focused landing pages that capture the 200k+ unrepresented MTD entrants.
- **Suggested bundle:** "MTD-Ready Practice". Includes website, local SEO/GBP, review engine, AI enquiry handler with MTD eligibility quiz, automated onboarding (engagement letter, AML/KYC via an ID-verification partner, 64-8/agent authorisation checklist), quarterly-record chase sequences via email, SMS and WhatsApp, and Companies House ID-verification tracker. Price £495–£995/month plus a £1–2k setup fee. That sits inside the £500–£2,500/month marketing spend band.
- **ROI logic:** if MTD-quarterly clients are priced at roughly £50–£100/month more than SA-only clients (an assumption; no fee survey was found), two or three new clients a month or a few hours a week of saved chasing would cover the fee.
- **SaaS potential:** high. "Quarterly-record and ID chasing for MTD clients" is a narrow, repeatable workflow with a hard deadline cadence (every quarter, with waves in 2027 and 2028). It could become a UK-specific add-on that integrates with Xero, FreeAgent and TaxCalc.
- **Regulatory risk:** low. ICAEW and ACCA require marketing to be legal, decent, honest and truthful (background knowledge, not verified this session). AML client due diligence (MLR 2017) and UK GDPR apply to document handling.
- **Reachability:** most practices are Ltd or LLP, so corporate-subscriber cold email is allowed under PECR with opt-out (see section 8). A meaningful share are sole practitioners or partnerships, which need consent (inference). Channels include AccountingWEB, Accountex (London and Manchester), ICAEW/AAT/ACCA local districts, r/UKAccountancy, and Xero/QuickBooks partner communities.
- **Scores:** Demand/pain 5; Ability to pay 4; Competition gap 3 (many tools and agencies, few done-for-you bundles); Reachability 5; Recurring/scalability 5; Regulatory risk 4.

### Gaps
- No survey data was found on what firms charge MTD clients, or on typical client lifetime value.
- No current pricing was found for accountant-specific web agencies (Thrive, Ignite, Accountants Alchemy) or for FutureFirm.
- AI features in Karbon, XPM, TaxCalc, Senta and IRIS were not researched this session.
- The exact firm count by size band (sole practitioner vs 2–10 staff) and the split by legal form for PECR were not found.

## 2. Solicitors / small law firms (conveyancing, family, wills & probate, immigration)

### Takeaway
There are about 8,900 regulated firms, with incorporated small firms growing and traditional sole practices shrinking. Price transparency is mandatory for conveyancing, probate and immigration, and compliance is poor. That gives a website bundle built for SRA Transparency Rules a sharp, compliance-led hook. Clio is bundling AI intake for free, which narrows the automation gap. Marketing, reviews and compliant pricing pages remain under-served for small firms (inference).

### Cited Findings
- 8,928 regulated firms at 31 October 2025, down from 9,147 a year earlier. The Gazette says the total hovers around 8,900, with 29 fewer firms in September 2026 than a year before — [SRA Authorisation report 2024/25](https://www.sra.org.uk/sra/research-publications/authorisation-annual-report-24-25/); [Law Gazette](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/commentary-and-opinion/supply-and-demand/5128400.article)
- Recognised sole practitioners number about 1,367, down from 4,030 in 2010. Incorporated companies hit a record 5,267 — [Law Gazette](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/commentary-and-opinion/supply-and-demand/5128400.article); [Global Legal Post (disputed decline)](https://www.globallegalpost.com/news/sole-practitioners-push-back-against-recent-decline-claims-91749380)
- 519 firms closed in the 12 months to March 2026 (about 61% ceased practising, about 21% merged) — [SRA firm closure breakdown](https://www.sra.org.uk/sra/research-publications//regulated-community-statistics/data/firm_closure_breakdown)
- 216,825 solicitors on the roll at end of April 2026 — [SRA population](https://sra.org.uk/sra/research-report/regulated-community-statistics/data/population_solicitors)
- The SRA Transparency Rules (in force 6 December 2018) require published price and service information for residential conveyancing, probate, motoring offences, employment tribunals and immigration (excluding asylum) **[older rule, still current]** — [SRA web sweep](https://www.sra.org.uk/sra/research-publications/web-sweep/)
- SRA web sweep of 500 firms: full compliance was highest for probate (29%) and conveyancing (25%) — [Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/web-sweep-of-500-firms-to-check-prices-are-published)
- The SRA has confirmed that firms will have to publish price information more widely. The scope and timing of the extension were not confirmed — [Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/firms-face-new-rule-to-publish-price-information-for-all-services/comment-page-1); [Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/law-firms-will-have-to-publish-certain-prices-but-not-complaints-data-sra-decides)
- Whito, Oct 2026: 26 of 36 probate price pages that quoted the court fee showed an outdated figure after it rose to £526 **[small, non-representative sample]** — [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/solicitors/solicitor-price-transparency/)
- Clio UK & I Legal Insights 2026: AI use is near-universal but integration lags. Flat fees cover 53% of matters, hourly billing 32% **[vendor]** — [Clio press](https://www.clio.com/uk/about/press/2026-uk-ireland-legal-insights-report/); [Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/associate-news/inside-the-uk-ireland-legal-insights-report-2026-ai-integration-and-the-firms-pulling-ahead)
- 71% of solo and 75% of small firms use AI, but fewer than a third grew revenue with it, and 86% of solos made no pricing changes **[vendor]** — [MLex/Law360](https://www.mlex.com/pulse/articles/2473002)
- Clio launched AI drafting, matter intake and billing at no extra charge. Clio Work standalone costs $199/user/month (US) **[vendor/aggregator]** — [pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/legaltech/news/clio-launches-aidriven-contract-drafting-intake-and-billing-tools-7120f6dd-eb79-4a57-8160-3576219dd603/)
- Clio UK from about £49–£79/user/month (sources conflict). LEAP pricing is not public — [Capterra Clio vs LEAP](https://www.capterra.com/compare/105428-147413/Clio-vs-LEAP); [Whito software table](https://whito.co.uk/for/solicitors/best-software-for-uk-solicitors/)

### Inferences
- **Gap:** case management systems (LEAP, Clio, Actionstep, Osprey) now ship intake and AI inside the platform, so selling generic automation directly against them is weak. The under-served space is a client-facing layer: SRA-compliant price pages kept current (court fees, Land Registry fees), GBP and reviews, an AI web enquiry triage that books calls and quotes from the published price list, and onboarding chasers for ID/AML, source-of-funds documents and probate asset lists. Conveyancing and probate firms have high enquiry volumes and price-shopping clients.
- **Bundle:** "Compliant Conveyancing/Probate Front Door". Price £600–£1,200/month. ROI: one conveyancing matter (fees typically around £1k–£2k, from background knowledge, unverified) per month covers it.
- **Regulatory risk:** moderate. The SRA Code governs publicity, and an AI that gives legal advice or quotes inaccurately creates exposure, so the AI must stay at triage level.
- **Reachability:** good. Most firms are incorporated (corporate subscribers), and the SRA register is public. Channels include Law Society, Legal Futures, Today's Conveyancer, and STEP for probate.
- **Scores:** Demand/pain 4; Ability to pay 4; Competition gap 3; Reachability 4; Recurring/scalability 4; Regulatory risk 3.

- LEAP and Actionstep AI features and UK pricing were not found.
- No data on law firm marketing spend per month.

## 3. Independent financial advisers & mortgage brokers (Consumer Duty)

### Takeaway
The pool is large: about 5,100 advice firms and about 35,000 mortgage advisers, heavily intermediated by networks. Consolidation and network principal firms often impose marketing compliance sign-off and supply their own tech, which limits a third-party bundle. Mortgage brokers have high enquiry volume and constant document chasing (payslips, bank statements), which suits automation. IFAs are richer but harder to sell to under FCA financial-promotion rules.

### Cited Findings
- FCA Financial Adviser Survey (April 2026): about 5,100 adviser firms, a fall of about 15% since 2021, with adviser numbers stable near 31,000. A further decline of about 5% is expected by 2028 — [Financial Planning Today](https://financialplanningtoday.co.uk/news/15-fall-in-adviser-firms-in-5-years-fca-survey); [Professional Paraplanner](https://professionalparaplanner.co.uk/advice-sector-shows-room-for-growth-says-fca/)
- Alternative FCA RMAR measure: 4,294 advice firms and 26,435 advisers (2025) — [Whito via search](https://whito.co.uk/?p=25886)
- NextWealth: hundreds of firms absorbed by PE-backed groups in five years — [Evidence Investor](https://www.evidenceinvestor.com/post/financial-advice-consolidation-uk). Seller-side claim that PE buyers rose from 42% of deals in 2020 to 75% in 2025 **[marketing source]** — [CT Acquisitions](https://www.ctacquisitions.com/sell-your-business/uk/wealth-ria/)
- The FCA says consolidation has not reduced access to advice, and its multi-firm review targets how firms consolidate — [Professional Adviser](https://www.professionaladviser.com/news/4528679/fca-consolidation-reduced-access-advice)
- Mortgage: individual mortgage advisers numbered 35,047 at 30 June 2026 — [Mortgage Solutions](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/07/30/mortgage-network-league-table-reveals-growth-divide-among-largest-firms/)
- AR framework principal firms fell from 2,568 (March 2025) to 2,431 (March 2026), with total ARs at 33,347 — [Mortgage Soup](https://mortgagesoup.co.uk/ar-market-consolidates-as-revenues-rise-despite-fewer-principal-firms/)
- Mortgage-only DA firms numbered 1,585 in 2025 — [Mortgage Solutions](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/news/2026/01/16/da-firms-declining-slightly-while-ar-market-more-stable-research-shows)
- Primis is the largest dedicated mortgage network with 1,018 AR firms. Stonebridge gained a net 29 firms in H1 2026 — [The Intermediary](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2026/07/mortgage-network-market-sees-continued-ar-movement-in-q2-network-consulting/)

### Inferences
- **Gap:** many brokers are ARs of networks that control compliance and often supply CRM (e.g., Mortgage Brain, Acre, Intelliflo; feature research not done this session). The bundle is best positioned as network-approved, or sold to DA firms (about 1,585 mortgage DA firms) and small IFAs. The strongest workflow is fact-find and document chasing plus product-expiry and remortgage follow-ups. Consumer Duty outcome monitoring of client communications is a possible value-add.
- **Bundle:** £400–£900/month. ROI: one extra mortgage case (proc fee plus broker fee, typically about £1k+, from background knowledge, unverified) per month.
- **Regulatory risk:** high-moderate. Financial promotions must be approved by an authorised person, and AI chat that drifts into advice is a regulatory problem.
- **Scores:** Demand/pain 4; Ability to pay 4 (IFA 5, brokers 3–4); Competition gap 3; Reachability 3 (networks are gatekeepers, though they are also a channel); Recurring/scalability 4; Regulatory risk 2.

### Gaps
- Primary FCA Consumer Duty and financial-promotions guidance was not fetched this session.
- No data on IFA or broker marketing spend.
- Intelliflo, Mortgage Brain and Acre AI features were not researched.

## 4. Insurance brokers

### Takeaway
A smaller, consolidating market of about 2,000–3,800 firms depending on definition. M&A is slowing, which leaves a stable independent cohort. Brokers rely on established platforms (Acturis, SSP, Applied), not researched here. This is a lower priority for a solo founder.

## 5. Recruitment agencies

A large pool of about 17,000 agencies (ONS), with high marketing literacy and established tooling (Bullhorn, Vincere, Firefish; not researched in depth). Pain sits in candidate and client acquisition and admin rather than in compliance-driven document chasing. The sector is cyclical and was soft in 2025–26.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Gap:** right-to-work and compliance document chasing for temp workers, plus AI candidate screening. Competition from ATS-native AI and many AI-recruitment start-ups is heavy. Willingness to pay is good in a boom and weak in a downturn.
- **Scores:** Demand/pain 3; Ability to pay 3; Competition gap 2; Reachability 4; Recurring/scalability 3; Regulatory risk 4.

## 7. IT MSPs

### Takeaway
There are about 12,000–12,800 MSPs with heavy PE roll-up activity (78% of IT services deals involved PE in Q3 2025). MSPs are technically capable and often build their own automation, and they resell to the same SMEs. They are better seen as a channel or partner (white-label the bundle to their SME clients) than as a target niche.

### Cited Findings

## 8. Cross-cutting regulation, reachability and ranking

### Takeaway
PECR permits cold B2B email to limited companies and LLPs (with identification and opt-out) but not to sole traders or non-Scottish partnerships. This favours niches dominated by incorporated firms: accountants (mostly), law firms (incorporated count at a record high) and MSPs. Overall ranking: 1) accountants/bookkeepers, 2) small law firms (conveyancing/probate), 3) mortgage brokers and IFAs (DA firms), 4) insurance brokers, 5) recruitment, 6) MSPs (better as a channel), 7) architects.

### Cited Findings
- PECR: sole traders and some partnerships are "individual subscribers" and need consent unless the soft opt-in applies. Limited companies, LLPs and Scottish partnerships are corporate subscribers. Senders must identify themselves and offer an opt-out — [ICO guide to PECR: email](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-pecr/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/what-are-the-rules-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail)
- Named employees' work addresses remain personal data under UK GDPR, so a legitimate-interests assessment is needed — [Harper James](https://harperjames.co.uk/article/b2b-marketing-and-gdpr-the-basics/)
- ICO PECR fines can reach £500,000 — [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/pecr-uk-privacy-and-electronic-communications-rules-explained/)
