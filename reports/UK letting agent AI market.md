# Win lettings agents on follow-up, not booking

UK letting agents need what the "never miss a lead" concept promises, and they already pay for versions of it. Where the plan needs to change is the offer. In 2026 the big CRMs (Alto, Street, Reapit, Apex27) built 24/7 AI enquiry handling into their own products, and Rightmove includes viewing booking, applicant pre-qualification and reminders in every lettings package. A solo founder selling "instant replies plus AI viewing booking" would be selling something many agents already own. The opening that is left is narrower but real. Most AI lead tools are built around sales valuations. Very few have been rewritten for the Renters' Rights Act, which took effect on 1 May 2026. Agents on older or smaller CRMs without native AI (Jupix, Expert Agent, Arthur, LetAdmin, Dezrez) are poorly served. And the documented failure is not only the first reply but the follow-up afterwards, plus the out-of-hours repair calls from tenants. The recommended target is **independent, lettings-led agencies managing roughly 150 to 1,000 properties, with one to three branches, registered as limited companies, on a CRM without strong native AI**. The recommended offer is a done-for-you, lettings-specific enquiry follow-up desk with Renters' Rights-compliant qualification, plus an out-of-hours tenant repair line. It should be priced at about £300 to £700 a month per branch on a rolling contract, sold with a self-run mystery shop of local agents' response times, and should plug into the agent's inbox, diary and existing booking tool rather than replace them. The ROI case rests on one number: a fully managed property earns the agent about **£2,000 a year in fees**, so roughly three extra landlords a year pays for the service. Read every figure with care. Most traction and results numbers in this market are vendor-reported, the best response-time studies are 5 to 10 years old, and the research worked from search summaries of trade press rather than full page reads.

## 25,000 fragmented agencies, squeezed by the Renters' Rights Act

The buyer pool is large and made up mostly of small firms. ONS-based analysis counts **25,665 active estate agency businesses in 2025**, up 2% on 2024, with about 4% growth forecast for 2026 ([Property Reporter](https://www.propertyreporter.co.uk/surge-in-uk-estate-agencies-sparks-tougher-competition.html); [Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/estate-agency-numbers-forecast-to-grow-4-in-2026/)). There are more than 14,500 agency brands ([Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/why-do-larger-estate-agencies-sell-fewer-homes-per-branch/)). The last published branch count, from Q4 2023, showed **14,544 active lettings branches** alongside 15,401 sales branches ([Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/?p=133334)). That data is old and paywalled, and no reliable count of lettings-only firms exists. Goodlord's industry survey describes the typical lettings business: **340 managed properties, 14 staff, and 54% of firms with five or fewer staff** ([Goodlord](https://www.goodlord.co/state-of-the-lettings-industry-report-2025)). That means thousands of owner-run firms with no in-house technical staff, which suits a done-for-you service. It also means each sale is small and the owner signs the cheque.

The economics give a clear ROI frame. Full management typically earns **10 to 15% of rent plus VAT** ([Landlord Studio](https://www.landlordstudio.com/uk-blog/letting-agents-fees)). Tenant-find earns 75 to 100% of the first month's rent ([CIA Landlords](https://www.cia-landlords.co.uk/advice/how-much-do-letting-agents-charge)). Average UK private rent was **£1,388 a month in June 2026** ([ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/privaterentandhousepricesuk/july2026)). At a 12% fee, one managed property is worth about £2,000 a year ex VAT, recurring for as long as the landlord stays. A let-only instruction is worth about £1,180 once. A sales instruction averages **£3,909 in fees** ([The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/agents-see-fees-earned-increase-as-sale-prices-rise/)), and agents already pay up to **£250 per successful instruction lead** from lead sources ([Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/?p=102543)). The arithmetic on these figures is ours.

Agents are under pressure on margins, which makes efficiency easier to sell. Goodlord found that **27% of agency revenue comes from tenancy renewals (37% in London)**. The Renters' Rights Act threatens that income because all tenancies are now periodic, and sole operators are the least prepared ([Letting Agent Today](https://www.lettingagenttoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2025/10/renters-reform-bill-could-slash-agency-revenue-by-27-figures/)). Agents are raising fees to make up the gap ([Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/agents-hike-fees-as-renters-rights-act-adds-to-landlord-pressures/)). Consolidation is ongoing. Private-equity platforms now buy lettings books rather than whole agencies ([FRP Advisory](https://www.frpadvisory.com/what-we-do/corporate-finance/insights/uk-lettings-estate-agency-market-consolidation-set-to-continue/)). Dwelly raised a **$170m Series B in July 2026** to buy letting agencies and run them on its own AI ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/dwelly-announces-170-million-series-b/amp/)). Every agency Dwelly buys leaves the buyer pool. The message that fits these conditions is "manage more properties per member of staff without hiring", not "better customer service".

## Slow replies are the best-evidenced pain, but the proof is aging

Out-of-hours demand is well evidenced. LightWork AI, a vendor, found that **43% of agency enquiries in July to September 2026 arrived outside 9 to 5 on weekdays** ([The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/proptech/nearly-half-of-agency-enquiries-arrive-out-of-hours-says-lightwork/)). Older Reapit research found 48% of applicant registrations arrived out of hours and that **about a third of online lettings enquiries were ignored**, mainly because nobody acted on them when the office reopened ([Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/nearly-one-third-of-leads-from-portals-ignored-by-agents/)). That research dates from roughly 2015 to 2017 and should not be quoted as current. The same goes for Rightmove's 2014 mystery shop of 213 letting agents, where **nearly 40% failed to answer a viewing request within 24 hours** ([Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/answer-within-24-hours-risk-losing-lead-rightmove-urges-agents/)), and for Homeflow's 2023 sales test, which measured an average first response of 229 business minutes ([Homeflow](https://www.homeflow.co.uk/posts/39-of-home-sellers-wait-for-an-estate-agents-response)). The research found **no independent UK lettings mystery shop from 2024 to 2026**. That gap is the reason for the founder's own mystery shop, covered below. Fresh, local data the founder collects will carry more weight with an agent than any vendor statistic.

The pains behind slow replies are admin overload and burnout. Goodlord reports that **76% of letting agents say admin limits their growth** ([Landlord Today](https://www.landlordtoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2026/04/landlords-unhappy-with-service-from-letting-agents-new-data/)). Alto's 2026 survey of 250 professionals found that **six in ten report burnout**, with mid-sized firms hit hardest ([Workplace Journal](https://workplacejournal.co.uk/2026/01/six-in-10-estate-agents-report-burnout-as-staffing-crisis-deepens-alto/)). On the landlord side, 67% are satisfied with agent communication overall, but **48% list lack of communication among their top frustrations** ([Landlord Today](https://www.landlordtoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2026/04/landlords-unhappy-with-service-from-letting-agents-new-data/)). Tenants face the same problem with repairs. Fixflo data shows **38% of repair issues and 45% of emergencies are reported out of hours** ([Fixflo](https://www.fixflo.com/solutions/lettings)), and out-of-hours contractor callouts cost up to 51% more ([Insurance Edge](https://insurance-edge.net/2025/10/20/property-maintenance-needs-call-out-cost-control/)). Regulation makes repairs triage more pressing. Awaab's Law for private landlords is under consultation until 18 December 2026 and would set statutory investigation and repair deadlines from 2027 at the earliest ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/clampdown-on-dangerous-homes-to-protect-more-tenants-and-families)). Time-stamped intake and escalation of repair reports would then become evidence that the agent met those deadlines, not just a convenience.

Some evidence is missing. The research found no measured UK viewing no-show rate, no data on time spent coordinating maintenance, and no survey of what agents would pay for AI tools. Attitudes to AI are mixed. Almost nine in ten larger agencies plan to adopt AI in 2026, while small independents lag and about a third feel nervous or unsure ([Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/estate-agents-turn-to-ai-amid-rising-compliance-pressures/)). In one survey, **76% of lettings agents had received no formal training on using AI in line with lettings law** ([Property Reporter](https://www.propertyreporter.co.uk/over-half-of-lettings-agents-have-no-plans-to-adopt-ai.html)). A nervous, untrained buyer is more likely to want a supervised service with a human in the loop than a self-serve tool.

## CRMs and Rightmove now give away the first reply and the booking

Competition changed sharply in 2026, and the change undercuts the original concept. In April 2026 Alto launched **Lead Flow**, which answers portal, WhatsApp and email enquiries 24/7, qualifies applicants and books viewings into Alto diaries. Alto claims it saves "150+ hours per branch per year" ([Alto Lead Flow PDF](https://staticassets.goldcast.io/public_images/organization/54d343e3-60cb-41ac-90bb-2b6b7e538c57/YhTknE70RMa4uujrsQ2x_Alto_Lead_Flow_one_pager_(2).pdf)). Alto says it is used by more than a third of UK agents, which is a vendor claim ([Alto](https://www.altosoftware.co.uk/blog/best-estate-agency-crm-software-in-2026-8-platforms-compared/)). Street's AI Call Handler answers calls 24/7 and books appointments, and Street says its AI tools come "with no extra subscriptions" ([Street](https://street.co.uk/product/ai)). Street also sells a "Zero-touch booking engine" that routes portal enquiries straight into the diary ([Street](https://live.street.co.uk/product/index/zero-touch-booking-engine)). Apex27's AgentAI receptionist is reported at **£150 a month plus 20p a minute** ([Cognumi](https://www.cognumi.co.uk/insights/best-ai-receptionists-uk)). Reapit's RAI was announced with lead scoring and maintenance workflows, and a marketplace for third-party AI agents was promised from June 2026. Neither could be confirmed live as of November 2026 ([Reapit](https://www.reapit.com/press-releases/reapit-sets-the-direction-for-the-ai-powered-estate-agency)).

Rightmove is the biggest threat to the "AI viewing booking" feature. Its **Viewings Manager is included in all lettings packages**. It asks pre-qualification questions, sends reminders 24 hours ahead and syncs with Outlook, Google and iCloud calendars ([Rightmove Hub](https://hub.rightmove.co.uk/viewings-manager)). Street, Reapit Bookings and LetAdmin also offer per-property pre-qualification rules ([Street](https://web2022.street.co.uk/blog/street-co-uk-gives-agents-more-control-over-online-bookings-with-new-pre-viewing-qualification-feature); [Reapit](https://www.reapit.com/platform/bookings); [LetAdmin](https://www.letadmin.com/blog/built-in-viewing-bookings-letting-agents)). Agents have been slow to adopt Rightmove's booking tool ([Online Marketplaces](https://onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/agents-hesitant-to-sign-up-for-rightmove-s-viewing-booking-tool)). That makes it something to connect to, not a reason to build a rival.

## The gap is lettings depth, follow-up and the CRMs left behind

The opening is in a few specific places, not in "AI for agents" generally. The first is **lettings-specific, Renters' Rights-aware conversations**. Most AI lead tools measure success by valuation leads captured, which is a sales metric. One commentary notes that almost none of the AI agents sold to letting agencies have been rewritten for the Act ([Fracas Digital](https://www.fracasdigital.com/blog/ai-agents-for-letting-agents-uk)). The Act changed what an enquiry conversation may contain. Agents must publish an asking rent and must not accept bids above it ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renting-out-your-property-guidance-for-landlords-and-letting-agents)). Requests to keep a pet cannot be unreasonably refused. Blanket bans on children or benefit claimants have been unlawful since 1 May 2026 ([Anthony Gold](https://anthonygold.co.uk/insight/the-renters-rights-act-2025-preventing-discrimination-in-the-private-rented-sector/)). Shelter advisers have already seen chatbots give false or misleading answers about the Act ([Silicon UK](https://www.silicon.co.uk/apac/letting-agents-ai-631122)). A tested set of compliant lettings scripts is a real product advantage, and it is also a safety requirement.

The second gap is **agents whose CRM has no native AI**. Alto's AI only works inside Alto and Street's only inside Street, and Reapit's is still rolling out. Agents on Jupix, Expert Agent, Dezrez, Arthur, LetAdmin, or just email and spreadsheets, are left out. Some agents also distrust their CRM vendor. Alto's Trustpilot reviews include complaints of a 56% price rise during a fixed-term contract and weaker support ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/altosoftware.co.uk)). There is a risk here. Zoopla is moving Jupix agents onto Alto, and the long-term plan is to merge its CRMs into one ([PrimeResi](https://primeresi.com/estate-agency-software-giant-overhauls-brand-structure/)). Jupix customers may therefore end up with Lead Flow within a year or two, so this segment is a window that will close, not a permanent home.

The third gap is **follow-up after the first reply**. Incumbent products focus on answering the first enquiry. The documented failures run further than that: one in five build-to-rent renters "ghosted" after a viewing (a second-hand report) ([blog summary](https://thats-business.blogspot.com/2026/02/one-in-five-renters-ghosted-after.html)), and 29% of tenants say they don't hear back ([Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/tenants-frustrated-by-lack-of-communication-from-letting-agents/)). Chasing applicants who went quiet, re-offering viewing slots, collecting post-viewing feedback and moving people towards referencing are all lightly served. The fourth gap is an **out-of-hours tenant line for small managing agents** that sorts emergencies from non-emergencies and logs the job in Fixflo or the PMS, rather than competing with Fixflo. The fifth is **automated landlord updates**, such as monthly portfolio summaries, compliance expiry dates and repair status. This is a plausible gap but unverified, and it depends on reading data from the PMS. Ranged against all of this are the risks: CRM bundling pushes prices down, many look-alike automation agencies are already pitching agents, and roll-ups shrink the pool of independent buyers.

## Integration v1 runs through the inbox, the diary and the CRM's front door

An independent vendor cannot pull Rightmove leads directly. Rightmove pushes enhanced lettings leads only into Alto and about five large CRMs, and has no public lead API ([Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/rightmove-integrates-enhanced-leads-with-agents-crm-system/)). Zoopla and OnTheMarket have lead APIs, but they are aimed at CRMs ([Zoopla developers](https://developers.zoopla.co.uk/reference); [OnTheMarket](https://expert.onthemarket.com/help-centre/lead-integration/)), and whether a third party can use them on an agent's behalf is unverified. The route that works for every agent is the inbox. Every portal enquiry also arrives as a notification email that can be parsed, either through a forwarding rule or by connecting the agent's Gmail or Microsoft 365 mailbox. That claim comes from vendors, and the email formats are undocumented and can change, so parsers need monitoring and alerts ([Softomate](https://www.softomatesolutions.com/blog/ai-automation-uk-letting-agencies-rightmove-tenancy-2026/)). Zapier's ready-made "Email Parser to new Street enquiry" template shows that the industry accepts this pattern ([Zapier](https://WWW.ZAPIER.COM/find-apps/s/streetcouk-enquiries)).

CRMs differ a lot in how open they are. **Street** lets an agent generate an Open API token themselves, has webhooks and a sandbox, and is how Lettie and LightWork AI connect ([Street](https://street.co.uk/integrations/lightwork-ai)). **Apex27** has an official n8n node covering contacts, listings, viewings and webhooks ([Apex27](https://apex27.co.uk/integration/n8n)). **Arthur**, a lettings PMS, offers its API "at no extra cost" ([Arthur](https://arthuronline.co.uk/connect/arthur-api)). **Reapit** bills per API call. LeadPro passes on **£0.0235 per call** to agents ([LeadPro](https://lead.pro/guides/reapit-integration)). Reapit's developer terms reportedly require at least **£200 a month in consumption per app**, which rules it out for v1 ([Reapit T&Cs](https://github.com/reapit/foundations-documentation/blob/master/developer-terms-and-conditions.md)). **Alto** requires approval through its Partner Hub and has been criticised as "pay to play", although that is an opinion in a comment thread ([Estate Agent Today](https://www.estateagenttoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2023/8/zoopla-backed-alto-launches-proptech-partner-hub)). Unified API providers such as Unified.to and Knit already offer Apex27 and Street connectors, which could save building each one separately ([Unified.to](https://unified.to/integrations/apex27)). For maintenance, Fixflo has an API and integrates with Arthur, Reapit, MRI and agentOS, but its access terms for a new vendor are unknown ([Fixflo](https://www.fixflo.com/integrations/lettings/arthur)).

There is one practical trap. Many agents keep viewings in the CRM diary, not in Google or Outlook, so writing bookings only to Google Calendar risks double-booking. Check each agent's setup before going live. Where the agent already uses Rightmove Viewings Manager or their CRM's booking engine, v1 should send the applicant that agent's own booking link after qualifying them, rather than running a separate diary. WhatsApp messages sent outside the 24-hour customer-service window need Meta-approved templates. That is general platform knowledge that was not verified in this research, and nor were Twilio pricing or UK SMS sender-ID rules.

## Price against a part-time assistant, and prove the value with one extra landlord

The price anchors fall into clear bands. AI receptionist start-ups charge about **£49 to £300 a month**, and Softomate lists £299 to £599 ([Voco](https://vocohq.co.uk/industries/property/lettings); [Softomate](https://www.softomatesolutions.com/ai-receptionist-estate-letting-agents/)). CRM AI add-ons cost about **£149 to £150 a month plus usage**. Human answering starts at **£49 a month** at Moneypenny ([Moneypenny](https://moneypenny.com/uk/telephone-answering-service)). UK virtual assistants cost about **£15 to £35 an hour, or £600 to £1,600 a month** for 10 to 20 hours a week. Those VA figures come from directory sites and are low-quality sources ([BizHub365](https://bizhub365.com/find/virtual-assistant/portsmouth/cost/); [AC Virtual Assistant](https://www.acvirtualassistant.co.uk/post/how-much-does-a-virtual-assistant-cost-in-the-uk-2026-guide)). Custom builds start from **£2,000 setup** ([Softomate](https://www.softomatesolutions.com/ai-receptionist-estate-letting-agents/)). A managed service that does more than an AI receptionist but costs less than a part-time VA sits between £300 and £700 a month per branch. That is a defensible middle point.

Contract terms are a second way to compete. Reapit reportedly requires a 12-month minimum, and AgentPro a 24-month minimum plus a £1,200 installation fee, while Apex27 wins business on rolling monthly terms ([Whito](https://whito.co.uk/for/estate-agents/best-software-for-uk-estate-agents/)). Agents complain on industry podcasts about "ever increasing fees and complicated contracts" ([Pocket Casts](https://pocketcasts.com/podcast/letting-estate-agent-podcast/8ee53f00-0642-0133-1b18-059c869cc4eb/suppliers-bullying-the-estate-and-lettings-industry-ep-2320/15568c54-6226-4b26-a0bf-a632bc48d90b)). Rolling monthly terms with a short paid pilot answer that complaint directly. Paying per outcome is also familiar in this market, since Yomdel historically charged about £10 per qualified lead ([Estate Agent Networking](https://estateagentnetworking.co.uk/generate-great-website-leads-247-just-by-chatting/)). A per-qualified-applicant top-up is therefore a credible option for agents who doubt the value.

Selling needs to avoid volume cold pitching. The NAEA has publicly described "proptech fatigue" (older coverage) ([The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/proptech-fatigue-naea/)), and cold sales calls to property firms were running about 10% above pre-Covid levels in 2022 ([Estate Agent Today](https://www.estateagenttoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2022/12/agents-hit-with-increasing-number-of-cold-sales-calls)). Outreach works better when it carries evidence about the agent's own business. A message such as "I enquired on your two-bed at 7.40pm on Tuesday and got a reply 14 hours later" does that. Lower-cost in-person channels include Propertymark's regional conferences, which have exhibitor options ([Propertymark](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/careers-learning/conferences/manchester/exhibitors.html)), peer leadership groups such as Property Academy ([Endole](https://open.endole.co.uk/insight/brand/84426-property-academy)), sector podcasts, and trade-press guest blogs. The industry mood favours AI that "strengthens human connection, not replaces it" ([Letting Agent Today](https://www.lettingagenttoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2026/05/ai-should-strengthen-human-connection-not-replace-it-call/)), so the pitch should talk about freeing staff time, not cutting jobs.

## Compliance shapes the scripts, the contract and the outreach list

Compliance touches three parts of the business. The first is outreach. Under PECR, cold B2B email to limited companies and LLPs is lawful without consent, as long as you identify yourself and offer an opt-out. **Sole traders and ordinary partnerships count as individuals**, so emailing them needs consent or the soft opt-in, and cold calls must be screened against both the TPS and CTPS registers ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/); [ICO telephone marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/telephone-marketing/)). Many small agents are sole traders, and since 5 February 2026 the maximum PECR fine has been **£17.5m or 4% of global turnover** under the Data (Use and Access) Act. That figure comes from a law-firm summary and should be checked on legislation.gov.uk ([HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/data/2026-posts/uk-data-protection-and-privacy-reform-goes-live-what-you-need-to-know)).

The second is the service contract. The agent is the data controller and the founder is the processor, so each client needs an **Article 28 contract**. It must cover acting only on documented instructions, staff confidentiality, security, approved sub-processors on equivalent terms, help with data-subject rights, deletion or return of data at the end, and audit rights ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/contracts-and-liabilities-between-controllers-and-processors-multi/what-needs-to-be-included-in-the-contract/)). The LLM provider, Twilio and the n8n host are all sub-processors, and any processing outside the UK needs a transfer mechanism.

The third is what the AI says. Property descriptions fall under the **DMCC Act 2024**, enforced by the CMA, which can fine up to 10% of turnover ([Propertymark](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/resource/material-information-guidance-withdrawn-as-the-digital-markets-competition-and-consumers-act-takes-over.html); [The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/?p=177146)). An AI that invents a council tax band or misstates a property detail exposes the agent to that regime. Qualification flows must not screen out applicants who have children or claim benefits. Councils can impose penalties of up to £7,000 under the Renters' Rights Act, and "No DSS" policies were ruled indirect discrimination under the Equality Act in 2020 ([Tozers](https://www.tozers.co.uk/insight/articles/no-more-no-children-what-landlords-and-letting-agents-need-to-know/); [Shelter](https://england.shelter.org.uk/media/press_releases/articles/no_dss_landmarkcourtrulingconfirmshousing_benefitdiscriminationisunlawful)). The Tenant Fees Act also rules out charging applicants a no-show deposit ([The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/agents-who-charge-tenants-30-for-viewings-risk-tenant-fees-act-prosecution/)). No regulator requires agents to disclose AI use to tenants. The Property Ombudsman's codes bind agents, not consumers, and complaints to the Ombudsman rose 50% in the first half of 2026 ([The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/regulation-law-news/consumers-wont-have-to-declare-use-of-ai-rules-ombudsman/)). Disclosing the AI and keeping complete logs is therefore both prudent and good evidence if a complaint arrives. The research did not cover: whether automated tenant screening counts as "significant" automated decision-making under the new UK GDPR Articles 22A to 22D, the expectations for a DPIA on call recording, ICO registration fees, and any TPO code clause on automated communications. A one-off review by a solicitor before the first paid client is worth the money.

## Recommendation

### Target independent lettings-led agencies on CRMs without AI

The primary segment is **independent, lettings-led agencies with full management books of roughly 150 to 1,000 properties and one to three branches**. They feel the most admin and burnout pain, have the most to lose from the end of renewal income, and lag on AI. Prioritise agencies that are **limited companies or LLPs**, which can be emailed cold lawfully, and that run **Jupix, Expert Agent, Dezrez, Arthur, LetAdmin, or email and spreadsheets**, because none of these has native AI lead handling. Use Companies House to check the legal form and the agency's website or job ads to identify its CRM. Treat agents on Street and Apex27 as a second wave. Their APIs are the easiest to work with, but their native AI means the offer there has to be lettings follow-up depth, not first response. Avoid Reapit-heavy corporates, Alto agents who have Lead Flow switched on, and franchise networks until there is proof to show them. Choose one region to start, so that local mystery-shop data, meetings in person and regional Propertymark events reinforce each other.

### Lead with lettings follow-up and an out-of-hours tenant line

| Feature | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Instant reply to portal and website enquiries, 24/7, by email plus SMS or WhatsApp | **Keep as the entry point** | Best-evidenced pain. Only a commodity for agents whose CRM has native AI, and the chosen segment's CRMs don't |
| Renters' Rights-compliant lettings qualification (move date, household, affordability against referencing criteria, pets handled lawfully, no bidding, no blanket exclusions) | **Lead with it** | Main differentiator. Most tools are sales-centric and not rewritten for the Act |
| Follow-up sequences: chasing quiet applicants, re-offering slots, post-viewing feedback, nudging towards referencing | **Lead with it** | Under-served. Directly addresses "ghosting" and lost lets |
| AI viewing booking with its own diary | **Drop as a feature; hand off to the agent's booking tool** | Rightmove Viewings Manager, Street, Reapit Bookings and LetAdmin already do this free or bundled. Send the agent's own booking link, or write to their calendar only where that calendar is their real diary |
| Booking reminders and no-show texts | **Drop** | Already in Rightmove Viewings Manager. No no-show data to sell against |
| Out-of-hours tenant repair line (WhatsApp and voice): triage, emergency escalation, time-stamped log, job created in Fixflo or the PMS | **Keep as the Core-tier add-on** | 38% of issues and 45% of emergencies arrive out of hours. Awaab's Law adds a compliance reason. Connect to Fixflo rather than compete with it |
| Weekly results report (response times, applicants qualified, viewings, repair tickets) | **Keep, as the retention and proof tool, not a selling feature** | It is what turns vendor-style claims into the agent's own evidence |
| Landlord updates | **Defer to v2** | Plausible but unverified gap. Needs PMS data access first |
| Generic 24/7 call answering for sales valuations | **Drop for the lettings segment** | Crowded (Moneypenny, Yomdel, CRM AI, start-ups). Valuation capture is what the incumbents measure |

### Position as the lettings follow-up desk, done for you and measured monthly

The one-line position is: **"A done-for-you lettings enquiry and tenant desk for independent agents. Renters' Rights-compliant, works with the CRM you already have, measured every week, on a rolling monthly contract."** Against CRM-native AI, it covers CRMs that have no AI, goes deeper on lettings, and is not tied to a vendor some agents distrust. Against Yomdel and Moneypenny, it reports outcomes (applicants qualified, viewings booked, repairs triaged) rather than messages taken, at a lower price than human cover with comparable scope. Against AI receptionist start-ups and generic automation agencies, it brings tested lettings scripts, setup and supervision by a person, and local response-time data. Disclose the AI in every conversation, offer a human handoff at any point, and describe the service as freeing negotiators for viewings and landlords.

### Price at £299 to £699 per branch, justified by about £2,000 per managed landlord

| Tier | Monthly (ex VAT, per branch) | Setup | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | £299 | £495 | Instant reply and compliant qualification on lettings enquiries (email plus SMS), follow-up sequences, handoff to the agent's booking link, weekly report |
| Core (lead offer) | £499 | £750 | Starter plus WhatsApp, out-of-hours tenant repair triage with escalation and logging, CRM write-back by email-to-CRM or API where available, monthly review call |
| Plus | £699+ | Quoted | Multi-branch, deeper API integration (Street, Apex27, Arthur, Fixflo), landlord update pilot, custom scripts |

These are recommendations, not observed market prices. They sit above the £49 to £300 AI-receptionist band and below the £600 to £1,600 part-time VA band. Offer a **30-day paid pilot**, for example setup waived and the first month at half price, then rolling monthly terms. Optionally add a per-qualified-applicant top-up above a volume cap, which protects the founder's running costs. The ROI case is simple arithmetic on cited figures. Core costs £5,988 a year. That equals about **three managed landlords' annual fees** (about £2,000 each at the ONS average rent and a 12% fee), five let-only fees (about £1,180 each), or 1.5 sales instructions (£3,909 each). It also costs less than 10 hours a week of a UK VA. The case should be sold with the agent's own numbers, from the mystery shop and pilot reports, not with vendor percentages. LLM, Twilio and hosting costs per enquiry were not researched. Measure them in the pilot before fixing the volume caps.

### Build v1 on the inbox, calendar links, Twilio and the open CRMs

Capture leads by forwarding or connecting the agent's lead inbox (Gmail or Microsoft Graph), parse portal and website notification emails in n8n, and alert on any parse failure. Reply and follow up through email, Twilio SMS and WhatsApp (with approved templates), and use the Claude API for conversation and qualification under strict prompts. Answer property questions only from a verified listing record, and escalate any question the listing data doesn't answer. Hand bookings off to the agent's existing booking link or real diary. Write records back to the CRM through its email-import address at first, then through the Street or Apex27 APIs (n8n node), the Arthur API, or a unified API, once a paying client uses one of them. Log repair tickets to Fixflo where the agent uses it, and otherwise to a shared inbox plus a time-stamped sheet. Defer Reapit Foundations, because of per-call charges and the reported £200-a-month minimum, and the Alto Partner Hub, because of the approval process. Revisit Reapit's AI-agent marketplace once it is confirmed live, as a possible distribution channel for the later SaaS.

## Conclusion

The research changes the founder's question. It is no longer whether agents need instant replies. It is which agents don't already have them, and what happens after the first reply. In 2026 the first response became a feature inside the agent's CRM and Rightmove package. The work still open is the work those tools treat as an afterthought: lettings conversations that comply with the new law, chasing applicants until they are referenced or ruled out, and handling a tenant's 2am leak in a way that will stand up under Awaab's Law. That favours a founder with lettings knowledge and patience for setup over one with the cleverest automation stack. It also defines the SaaS endgame. The workflow worth productising is the one that keeps working when the CRM adds its own AI.

The second implication concerns proof. This market runs on vendor percentages and response-time studies from 2014 and 2017. A solo founder who generates their own measured, dated, local data has an asset that funded competitors have not bothered to build. That data comes first from a mystery shop and then from pilot reports. It works as a sales hook, as content, and later as a moat. The window is real but limited. Zoopla is moving Jupix agents onto Alto, Reapit's agent marketplace is coming, and roll-ups keep buying independents. The next 12 to 18 months matter more than the long-term SaaS plan.
