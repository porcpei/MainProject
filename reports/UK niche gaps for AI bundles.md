# Sell MTD client-chasing to accountants first

Across about 30 UK small-business niches scored on one scale, the best opening for a solo technical founder selling a bundled recurring service is **small accountancy and bookkeeping practices facing Making Tax Digital for Income Tax**. The best bundle there is built around automated client-information chasing and onboarding, with website, reviews and enquiry capture added on top. The runner-up is **MCS-certified heat pump and solar/battery installers**, who have the highest job values, a small and countable buyer list, and record grant demand, but who also carry policy risk and need paid-ads skill. **Wedding and event venues** and **private nurseries** come next, and **UK letting agents**, the benchmark from earlier research, land mid-table at 21 out of 30. They do not lack pain. The problem is that their CRMs and Rightmove now give away the first reply and the viewing booking. The accountancy case rests on a statutory calendar: about **780,000 sole traders and landlords were mandated from April 2026 and a further 970,000 from April 2027**, with a £20k threshold following in April 2028. The top obstacle accountants report to on-time quarterly updates is **getting information from clients (67%)**, which is exactly the work an automated chaser does. Across every niche the evidence points the same way. Buyers pay for revenue capture and follow-up that fit their own workflow, and for admin that a deadline forces on them. Websites, basic SEO and a standalone "AI receptionist" are commodities. Read this report with three caveats. Every score is judgement applied to the notes, not measured data. Most market and pricing figures are vendor-reported or come from search-result summaries rather than primary pages. And the single most important ROI input for the top pick, what practices charge MTD clients, was not found and must be validated in discovery calls.

## Thirty niches on one scale: accountants and installers lead, letting agents sit mid-table

Six research notes scored their own niches out of 30, but each researcher calibrated differently. This report re-scores every niche on a single rubric, using 1 to 5 per criterion with 5 always best. **Demand/pain** measures documented, current pain that the bundle addresses. **Ability to pay** compares the niche's existing spend and value per customer against a £300 to £1,500 monthly bundle. **Competition gap** runs from 5 (nothing serves this) through 3 (specialist tools and agencies exist but nobody combines them) to 2 (incumbents bundle the valued features natively) and 1 (saturated). **Reachability** scores 5 for a public, countable list of mostly incorporated firms plus trade events, 3 for niches that are heavily sole traders or behind gatekeepers, and 2 for procurement or committee buying. **Recurring/scalability** reflects how sticky the subscription is and how similar the workflow is from client to client, which governs the SaaS path. **Regulatory risk** scores 5 for low risk. These scores are the author's judgement applied to the cited notes. They are not survey data, and a one-point difference is within the margin of error.

Reachability depends heavily on UK outreach law. Under PECR, limited companies, LLPs and Scottish partnerships are "corporate subscribers" and can receive cold marketing email as long as the sender identifies itself and offers an opt-out. **Sole traders and other partnerships are "individual subscribers" and need consent** ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing/business-to-business-marketing); [ICO email rules](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-pecr/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/what-are-the-rules-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail)). Named work addresses are still personal data under UK GDPR, so a legitimate-interests assessment is needed ([Harper James](https://harperjames.co.uk/article/b2b-marketing-and-gdpr-the-basics/)). The stakes rose in 2026. Under the Data (Use and Access) Act, maximum PECR fines rose from £500k to UK GDPR levels of **£17.5m or 4% of turnover** from 5 February 2026, according to a law-firm summary that should be checked against legislation.gov.uk ([Hill Dickinson](https://www.hilldickinson.com/our-view/articles/data-use-and-access-act-changes-to-uk-data-protection-law-come-into-effect/)). Older summaries still quote £500k and are now out of date. The result is that cold email works for niches made up mostly of incorporated firms: accountants, law firms (incorporated companies are at a record 5,267 against about 1,367 recognised sole practitioners ([Law Gazette](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/commentary-and-opinion/supply-and-demand/5128400.article))), MCS installers, dental and vet practices, and letting agents. Plumbers, electricians, therapists, lash techs, driving instructors and about half of roofers have to be reached by phone (screened against TPS/CTPS), in person, through merchants and communities, or via Instagram DMs.

Several choices behind the table need explaining. Accountants edge out installers on **recurring/scalability**. MTD imposes a quarterly cadence on every client indefinitely, while installer demand rises and falls with grant budgets and the bundle's value there depends mostly on ad performance. Nurseries drop from the 25 their note gave them to 24 because the evidence works against parent-enquiry marketing. **49% of group-based providers have introduced waiting lists because of staffing** ([Nursery World](https://nurseryworld.co.uk/news/article/long-read-nursery-world-survey-most-providers-unable-to-meet-demand-for-free-twos-offer)), so the binding constraint is recruitment. The bundle that sells there is a hiring funnel, not more parent leads. Dental has the deepest wallets but scores 2 on gap. Agencies already charge **£800 to £3,000+ a month** ([Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/uk-dentist-marketing-costs/)), AI receptionists already claim integration with Dentally, SOE and R4 ([Viva](https://www.getviva.ai/ai-receptionist-software/)), and Dentally launched an AI layer in June 2026 ([Scottish Dental](https://www.sdmag.co.uk/2026/06/26/dentally-launches-dentally-pulse-ai-powered-intelligence/)).

Letting agents deserve a fair hearing, because they are the most thoroughly researched niche in this set. Their pain is real and well documented. Fixflo data shows **38% of repair issues and 45% of emergencies arrive out of hours** ([Fixflo](https://www.fixflo.com/solutions/lettings)), and **76% of agents say admin limits growth** ([Landlord Today](https://www.landlordtoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2026/04/landlords-unhappy-with-service-from-letting-agents-new-data/)). Their ROI anchor is clean: a managed property is worth about £2,000 a year at the ONS average rent of £1,388 a month and a 12% fee ([ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/privaterentandhousepricesuk/july2026)). What sinks their gap score is 2026 product news. Alto's Lead Flow answers and books 24/7 ([Alto](https://staticassets.goldcast.io/public_images/organization/54d343e3-60cb-41ac-90bb-2b6b7e538c57/YhTknE70RMa4uujrsQ2x_Alto_Lead_Flow_one_pager_(2).pdf)), Street's AI tools come "with no extra subscriptions" ([Street](https://street.co.uk/product/ai)), and Rightmove's Viewings Manager is included in every lettings package ([Rightmove Hub](https://hub.rightmove.co.uk/viewings-manager)). The segment without native AI, such as Jupix users, is shrinking as Zoopla moves Jupix onto Alto ([PrimeResi](https://primeresi.com/estate-agency-software-giant-overhauls-brand-structure/)). One bias runs through the whole table. Deeper research usually uncovers more competitors, so the lightly researched niches are probably over-scored on gap compared with letting agents. Accountants' gap score of 3 has not yet been tested against the AI features in Karbon, Xero Practice Manager, TaxCalc or IRIS, none of which were researched.

## Missed enquiries and follow-up earn the money; websites are table stakes

The clearest evidence on which components SMBs value comes from public vertical platforms. Thryv's SaaS revenue grew **34.2% to $461.0M in 2025**, while its legacy Marketing Services line fell **32.6% to $324.0M** ([Thryv](https://www.thryv.com/news/thryv-achieves-saas-revenue-growth-of-34-in-full-year-2025-shifts-focus-to-ai-enabled-market-sell-grow-platform-to-empower-smbs/)). Small businesses keep paying for software that runs their day and drop marketing sold on its own. The UK version of that lesson is Yell, which became Hibu and went through a lender-controlled restructuring in 2013 ([VCPost](https://www.vcpost.com/articles/12077/20130725/uk-yellow-pages-operator-hibu-lenders-agree-restructuring-deal.htm)). Podium reports **more than $150M in AI-agent ARR within 2.5 years** ([RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/podium-ai-agents-150m-arr)), but the figure is company-reported and the definition is unclear. It still suggests that AI enquiry handling is what SMBs will currently pay extra for. Weave's net revenue retention of 93% and gross revenue retention of 89% in Q4 2025 ([Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/313943/weave-announces-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-financial-results)) show that even sticky vertical platforms lose about a tenth of revenue a year. SMB SaaS benchmarks of **3 to 7% monthly logo churn** ([Vena](https://venasolutions.com/blog/saas-churn-rate); [Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/saas-churn-benchmarks-2026)) mean a solo service needs setup fees, ownership of the client's lead pipeline, and monthly ROI reports to keep customers.

Revenue capture is the top-valued component, and the evidence for it is broad but mostly vendor-adjacent. In a Fix Radio survey of more than 220 tradespeople, **34% had lost work by not answering the phone** ([Trade2Base](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/trade-business-call-handling-uk)). Moneypenny found that **69% of callers will not leave a voicemail** ([Moneypenny](https://www.moneypenny.com/uk/resources/news/small-business-call-report/)), and BT Business puts the cost of missed calls to UK firms at £3.7bn a year ([Irish News](https://www.irishnews.com/news/business/missed-phone-calls-have-cost-local-businesses-nearly-3000-this-year-3MJCU4WMCNG45HK4R2RDUIMLQI/)). Follow-up is the next most valuable component and the most consistently neglected. Wedding venues follow up an average of **about 1.4 times when 3 to 4 touches are often needed** ([Wedinspire](https://www.wedinspire.com/articles/business/guide-to-follow-up-emails/)). About a third of online lettings enquiries were historically ignored ([Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/nearly-one-third-of-leads-from-portals-ignored-by-agents/)), although that data dates from around 2015 to 2017. Reviews and Google Business Profile are gaining value because AI Overviews cut position-one organic click-through by about **58%** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update)). When search engines answer on the results page, local visibility depends on review volume and profile quality rather than on the website.

The same evidence shows where value collapses. A standalone AI receptionist is now cheap: Fasthosts starts at **£39 a month** ([Fasthosts](https://www.fasthosts.co.uk/blog/how-much-does-an-ai-receptionist-cost/)). Incumbent platforms are adding receptionists to their own products, including Jobber ([Jobber](https://getjobber.com/features/ai-receptionist/)), Powered Now ([Powered Now](https://powerednow.com/receptionist/)), Alto and Street, and Clio now ships AI intake at no extra charge ([pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/legaltech/news/clio-launches-aidriven-contract-drafting-intake-and-billing-tools-7120f6dd-eb79-4a57-8160-3576219dd603/)). Websites sell for **£85 a month with no setup fee** ([OneBase Media](https://onebasemedia.co.uk/trail-pricing/)), and booking engines are close to free in beauty (Fresha from £14.95 a month ([Whito](https://whito.co.uk/beauty-salons/tools/salon-booking-software-costs/))) and in holiday lets. Price sensitivity is high overall: 58% of SMEs spend under £250 a month on marketing ([Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/uk-marketing-budget-by-revenue/)), from a survey with an undisclosed sample, and only 22% feel "very confident" that their marketing works ([Constant Contact via Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/13447832745108480)).

That gives a five-level value hierarchy. Highest is enquiry capture and qualification wired into the niche's own workflow, using niche scripts (BUS eligibility for heat pumps, Renters' Rights-compliant questions for lettings, MTD eligibility for accountants). Second is multi-touch follow-up and reactivation. Third is review generation and GBP. Fourth is niche admin automation where a deadline forces the work: document chasing, onboarding, recall. Fifth, and commodity, are the website and basic SEO. There is one important exception. A website becomes valuable again when regulation dictates what it must show: a CMA-compliant vet price list, a funeral director's Standardised Price List, or SRA price pages that stay current. In an SRA sweep of 500 firms, full compliance was only **29% for probate and 25% for conveyancing** ([Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/web-sweep-of-500-firms-to-check-prices-are-published)). Price the bundle on levels one to four and include level five. On cost, the notes found no study of AI-native agency margins. Voice minutes and WhatsApp messages (billed per message since 1 July 2025 ([Twilio](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/30304057900699-Notice-Changes-to-WhatsApp-s-Pricing-July-2025))) scale with usage, so every tier needs caps or pass-through pricing.

## Five bundles priced against what each niche already pays

Each bundle below is the author's recommendation built on the notes. The prices are not observed market rates, and the ROI arithmetic uses the figures cited alongside it plus clearly stated assumptions.

### Accountants: the MTD-ready practice desk

The pain is documented by small surveys. In an Accountants Therapy survey of 142 respondents, **only 48 submitted all their clients' quarterly updates on time**. The obstacles were client information (67%), practice capacity (46%) and digital record keeping (29%) ([AccountingWEB](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/232190)). In a TaxCalc survey, **48% said lack of capacity limits growth and 47% plan to raise prices for MTD clients** ([AccountingWEB](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/231852)), and 59% of accountants say at least half of their income tax clients are not yet digital ([Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en-gb/expert-insights/non-digitalised-clients-key-challenges-mtd-income-tax)). Existing tools are self-serve and priced per seat in US dollars: Karbon costs $59 to $89 per user per month ([Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/karbon-pricing-guide)), Ignition starts at $49 ([GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/finance-accounting-software/a/practice-ignition/)), and Content Snare's tiers are $29 to $99, with sources disagreeing ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/content-snare/pricing)). The firms with the least capacity are the ones least able to set those tools up. Marketing spend gives the price ceiling: Whito estimates that a practice under £500k turnover spends **£500 to £2,500 a month** ([Whito](https://whito.co.uk/accountants/research/uk-accountant-marketing-costs/)), although that is an agency-published estimate that was seen only in a search summary.

The bundle should lead with the chaser. It sends quarterly-record requests by email, SMS and WhatsApp, escalating until the client responds, and collects receipts and bank data in a client portal with a status board for the practice. It includes onboarding automation (engagement letter, an AML/KYC partner, an agent-authorisation checklist), a Companies House ID-verification tracker, and a website with an MTD eligibility quiz that books calls from the 200,000+ unrepresented entrants. GBP and reviews complete it. A sensible structure is **Starter at about £395 a month** (chaser plus onboarding), **Core at about £695** (adding website, MTD landing pages, AI enquiry handling, GBP and reviews) and **Growth at £995 or more** (adding paid search). Each tier takes a £1k to £2k setup fee. There is no sourced fee uplift, so the ROI case rests on stated assumptions. If an MTD client pays £50 to £100 a month more than a self-assessment-only client, as the professional-services note assumed, two or three new MTD clients a month cover Core. Alternatively, if the chaser saves five staff hours a week at an assumed £30 an hour, that is about £7,800 a year against Core's £8,340, before counting any client acquisition. The hours and rates are the assumptions the discovery calls have to test.

### MCS installers: the installer growth OS

Demand is at record levels, and the buyer list is small, public and concentrated. **1,789 MCS companies were registered for BUS at the end of March 2026, and the ten largest took over 36.5% of applications** ([Ofgem](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/transparency-document/boiler-upgrade-scheme-annual-report-april-2025-march-2026)). The other roughly 1,750 firms are the natural buyers. Solar and battery installs added about 150,000 and 36,000 certified installations in the first half of 2026 ([CIBSE Journal](https://www.cibsejournal.com/?p=47081)), and 92% of the batteries went into homes that already had solar, which gives installers a ready upsell base. The standard air-source grant is £7,500 ([Eco Experts](https://www.theecoexperts.co.uk/blog/boiler-upgrade-scheme)). Payaca, Spruce (which claims 800+ installers), Zigaflow and Reonic cover CRM and design ([Payaca](https://payaca.com/uk/industries/heat-pump-installers); [Spruce](https://www.spruce.eco/demo-video-b)). Agencies cover ads. Nobody visibly sells AI pre-qualification together with done-for-you growth.

The bundle should run at **£995 to £1,495 a month plus £1.5k setup, with ad spend extra**. It includes a conversion site with a grant and savings calculator, service-area GBP pages and reviews, Meta and Google ads management, and an AI call and chat agent that checks owner-occupier status, property type, EPC, current fuel and BUS eligibility before booking a survey. Proposal follow-up, post-install review and referral sequences, and BUS document chasing complete it. On the ROI side, the notes estimate one extra install a month at roughly £2k to £4k gross margin, which is the author's judgement since no primary job-value source was found, so the bundle pays for itself on one job. The pitch to ECO4-dependent firms writes itself before the 31 December 2026 cliff. The risk is that results depend on paid-ads performance, which is a skill the founder must have or buy in, and on grant budgets the founder does not control.

### Wedding and event venues: speed-to-lead and nurture

This is the cleanest ROI story with no regulatory friction. Venue hire averages **£6,040** within a total wedding spend of about £20,600 to £22,000 ([Bridebook](https://bridebook.com/uk/article/national-wedding-survey-2018); [Hitched](https://www.theknotww.com/press-releases/the-average-cost-of-a-wedding-in-2026-around-21990-according-to-hitched)). Most venues do not track first-response time ([Coco Wedding Venues](https://cocoweddingvenues.co.uk/our-members-community/understanding-why-your-conversion-rate-has-changed-with-customer-journey-maps/)), and they follow up about 1.4 times. A bundle at **£600 to £900 a month plus £750 to £1,200 setup** combines an AI enquiry agent across web, Instagram, WhatsApp and parsed Bridebook/Hitched emails, a 3 to 4-touch nurture sequence, show-round booking, open-day campaigns and reviews. It costs £7k to £11k a year and pays back on one or two extra weddings. Bridebook's URL suggests an older survey page, so confirm the figure is current. The weaknesses are a small, uncounted market and seasonality. Both shrink if the vertical is widened to golf clubs (about 1,735 in England ([The Golf Business](https://thegolfbusiness.co.uk/2025/04/how-many-golfers-and-golf-clubs-are-there-in-england/))), hotel events and corporate venues, all of which use the same enquiry-to-show-round workflow.

### Independent vets: CMA-ready practice front door

Corporate groups own about 60% of practices, and **corporate prices were 18.3% higher than independents** ([CMA working papers](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67a3e65108d82b458c553ce9/Overview_of_our_working_papers.pdf); [FIVP](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/664e08c7993111924d9d3a2b/The_Federation_of_Independent_Veterinary_Practices__FIVP_.pdf)). The CMA's final remedies require standard price lists, care-plan information and practice comparison ([GOV.UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/what-veterinary-businesses-and-vets-need-to-do-following-the-cmas-final-vets-report)). Independents therefore have both a compliance task and a marketing opening: "transparent and cheaper". A bundle at **£695 to £1,195 a month** combines a CMA-compliant price-list website, GBP and reviews, health-plan acquisition campaigns, non-clinical AI phone triage to booking, and vaccine and parasiticide reminders. Competition from VetRec (integrated with ezyVet) and Provet exists but is narrow ([ezyVet](https://www.ezyvet.com/integration/vetrec-ai-receptionist)). The buyer pool of about 2,000 independents is small, the in-force dates of the Orders were not verified, and the ROI rests on pet lifetime value, which was not sourced.

### Nurseries: the hiring and waitlist desk

Nurseries need staff more than enquiries. **About 70% of 714 providers lacked staff to run at full capacity; the average nursery needed 3.3 more staff to open 13.1 more places** ([Nursery World](https://www.nurseryworld.co.uk/articles/seven-in-ten-nurseries-dont-have-enough-staff-to-operate-at-maximum-capacity-warns-ndna)). A full-time under-two place costs just under **£149 a week** ([NDNA on Coram](https://ndna.org.uk/coram-childcare-survey-2026-findings/)), or roughly £7k a year (the author's arithmetic). The bundle should therefore lead with recruitment: careers page, job-board posting, applicant chatbot, interview booking and apprentice pipeline. Waitlist nurture that re-offers places by date and room comes next, then GBP, reviews and an AI parent-enquiry line. It sells at **£299 to £499 a month per site** and integrates with Famly rather than replacing it. One hire that unlocks even a few places pays for a year. Ability to pay is the constraint, because 85% of providers say funding for 3 to 4-year-olds is below cost ([EYA](https://under5.eyalliance.org.uk/articles/news-be22)), and Famly's own enquiry module ([Famly](https://famly.co/lead/boost-nursery-enquiries)) overlaps with the parent side. Domiciliary care has the same recruitment-led shape at £400 to £800 per branch, but care-specific recruitment tools were not checked, and that competitor check is essential before entering.

## Recommendation: MTD practice desk first, installer growth OS second

### Top pick: small accountancy practices, sold the MTD-ready practice desk

Accountants win for five reasons that fit this founder's profile. First, the demand is statutory, dated and repeats every quarter, with a 970,000-person cohort arriving in April 2027 and another in April 2028. Second, the valued component is admin automation and integrations with Xero, FreeAgent and TaxCalc, which is where a computer science degree gives an edge over the many marketing-led AI agencies. Third, most targets are incorporated and reachable by lawful cold email through public registers, although the split by legal form was not found and should be checked firm by firm on Companies House. Fourth, the workflow is the same in every practice, which makes the SaaS path short. Fifth, incumbents sell self-serve tools rather than doing the work. The main risks are that the competition gap has not been tested against practice-management AI, that accountants are conservative and slow to buy, and that the fee uplift behind the ROI is assumed. The pitch also avoids the field where competitors are most crowded: it does not lead with "AI receptionist".

### Runner-up: MCS heat pump and solar/battery installers

Installers offer the highest bundle price, a countable list of about 1,800 named firms, record demand, and an ECO4 cliff that creates urgency. They rank second because their value depends on paid-ads results and on grant budgets set in Whitehall, and because Payaca and Spruce could add AI qualification. A founder who already has, or can partner for, ads skill could reasonably reverse the order. Wedding and event venues are the low-risk third option: the quickest to validate, with no regulatory complexity, but with the smallest SaaS ceiling.

### Why the top pick beats letting agents, and where it does not

The two niches score 25 and 21 on the same rubric. Accountants win on **competition gap** (3 against 2) and on **certainty of forced demand**. The MTD waves get bigger through 2028, while the letting-agent window closes as Alto and Street switch on native AI and Zoopla migrates Jupix. Accountants also make a better wedge for SaaS, because a quarterly chaser keeps working whatever the practice software adds, provided that software does not ship the same feature. Letting agents still beat accountants in two ways that should not be ignored. Their ROI anchor of about £2,000 per managed property comes from ONS rent data, while the accountants' fee uplift is an assumption. Their market has been examined in much more depth, so the 21 is a more reliable score than the 25. If discovery calls show that Karbon, Xero Practice Manager or TaxCalc already chase MTD records well enough, the accountants' gap falls to 2 and the two niches are effectively tied. A lettings-led bundle is then a reasonable fallback. There is also a link between them: landlords are an MTD cohort, so a practice desk could later serve agents' landlord clients.

### Path to SaaS: service, then AI-native service, then product

Stage one is a productised service for the first 10 to 30 practices in one region, run on a low-code stack. Options include GoHighLevel's **$497-a-month SaaS Pro tier** for CRM, sites and messaging ([Webdew](https://www.webdew.com/blog/gohighlevel-saas-mode)), with the caveat that generic GHL agencies are widely described as saturated ([GHL Scale Up](https://www.ghlscaleup.com/services/gohighlevel-saas-mode)), or n8n with Twilio and an LLM API for the chaser. Stage two is an AI-native service. AI agents do the chasing, sort the documents and flag missing items, a human supervises exceptions, and the founder publishes named before-and-after metrics such as on-time submission rate and hours saved, which no vendor in this space publishes independently. Stage three, after about 10 clients, rebuilds the one workflow that is identical across clients, almost certainly the quarterly-record and ID-verification chaser, as a self-serve UK product with Xero, FreeAgent and TaxCalc integrations. This is the wedge-then-expand pattern Weave and Podium followed. Weave's retention dip, partly attributed to newer vertical cohorts, is a warning to resist adding niches too early. Marketing services stay as an upsell, never as the core.

## Conclusion

The cross-niche view changes the question from "which niche has the most pain" to "which pain incumbents cannot absorb". Missed calls are the best-documented pain in UK small business, and they are also exactly what CRMs, booking platforms and £39-a-month tools now handle by default. That is why letting agents, dental practices and trades score lower than their pain would suggest. The durable openings are in work that a statute or regulator imposes on a recurring schedule and that existing software leaves to the customer: chasing clients for quarterly tax records, keeping regulated price pages current, and logging repair responses against legal deadlines. For a technical founder, that favours selling the admin no one wants to do over selling marketing.

The second lesson is that timing matters more than total market size. The 2027 MTD cohort, the ECO4 cliff on 31 December 2026, and the private rented sector extension of Awaab's Law all have dates within the next 18 months, and a solo founder with two or three dated case studies by spring 2027 will beat better-funded generalists to those buyers. The ranking is only as good as its weakest assumption, though. For the top pick, the founder can test that assumption in 20 phone calls before writing a line of code.
